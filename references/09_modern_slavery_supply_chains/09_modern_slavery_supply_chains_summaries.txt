===== PAPER: 09_01_A systematic literature review of modern slavery in supply chain =====
This paper conducts a systematic literature review of modern slavery in supply chain management, addressing the growing academic and legislative interest in combating forced labor across global supply chains. The authors extend Gold et al.'s (2015) conceptual model by introducing new factors including additional external stakeholders, prevention, and remediation as discrete phases in managing modern slavery risks. Key findings include the identification of research gaps related to Industry 4.0 technologies, stakeholder theory applications, and the over-reliance on secondary data such as modern slavery statements. The study is relevant to legal NLP research as it highlights the need for data-driven approaches, including AI and data analytics, to inject visibility into supply chain processes and detect modern slavery indicators.

===== PAPER: 09_02_Modern slavery challenge =====
This seminal paper draws attention to the challenges modern slavery poses to supply chain management, providing a foundational conceptual framework for the field. The authors define contemporary slavery, explore knowledge exchange with other disciplines, and discuss management tools for detecting slavery as well as suitable company responses after detection. They find that new indicator systems must be developed considering specific social, cultural, and geographical contexts, and that multi-stakeholder partnerships and supplier development are effective post-detection responses. This work is relevant to legal NLP and information extraction as it establishes the conceptual basis for identifying slavery indicators in supply chain documentation, including Brazil's "dirty list" and legislative compliance texts.

===== PAPER: 09_03_Modern Slavery Disclosure Regulation and Global Supply Chains- Insights from Stakeholder Narratives on the UK Modern Slavery Act =====
This paper problematizes the UK Modern Slavery Act's transparency and disclosure requirements by interviewing anti-slavery activists and experts to explore the Act's limitations in eliminating slavery from global supply chains. Drawing on regulatory theory and notions of normativity, the study finds limited confidence among activists regarding the Act's effectiveness, noting that corporate self-interest and regulatory capture constrain meaningful transparency. The research reveals that limited disclosure requirements, restricted entity coverage, and absent supply chain traceability provisions hinder the Act's normativity. This work is relevant to legal NLP as it demonstrates how textual analysis of corporate modern slavery statements requires understanding the gap between disclosed information and actual supply chain practices.

===== PAPER: 09_04_The Role of Supply Chains in the Global Business of Forced Labour =====
This paper explores how global supply chains facilitate forced labor, examining the structural dynamics that give rise to labor exploitation across product and labor supply chains. Drawing on empirical research funded by the UK ESRC, LeBaron argues that supply chain design and management fundamentally shape whether decent work flourishes, highlighting how cost-reduction pressures and outsourcing create vulnerability to forced labor. The study calls for business scholars to develop rigorous research methods connecting to existing cross-disciplinary research on forced labor. Its relevance to legal NLP lies in establishing the contextual understanding necessary for extracting information from legal documents about criminal labor exploitation in supply chains.

===== PAPER: 09_05_Detecting and remediating modern slavery in supply chains  a targeted audit approach =====
This paper investigates modern slavery detection and remediation through action research in the textiles and fashion industry, presenting the first empirical account of a targeted audit process at a high-risk supplier in South East Asia. The authors find that targeted audits investigating the end-to-end recruitment process through parallel management and worker interviews are more effective at identifying modern slavery indicators than standard social audits. The remediation process involves collaboration with local NGOs to empower workers and develop suppliers through ongoing day-to-day engagement rather than resource-intensive periodic audits. This work is relevant to legal NLP and information extraction as it identifies specific indicators of modern slavery (recruitment fees, passport retention, wage withholding) that could serve as named entities in automated document analysis of judicial decisions involving labor exploitation.
