===== PAPER: 10_01_A survey of sentiment analysis in the Portuguese language =====
This paper surveys the state of the art in sentiment analysis specifically for the Portuguese language, addressing the challenge that most NLP resources and tools have been developed for English despite Portuguese being among the top five languages on the web. The author categorizes and describes approaches to sentiment analysis tasks, as well as supporting resources including NLP tools, lexicons, corpora, ontologies, and datasets available for Portuguese. The survey highlights that multilingual approaches using automatic translation often fail to capture language-specific knowledge expressed through slang, idioms, and local culture. This work is relevant to legal NLP in Portuguese as it documents the linguistic resources and challenges that also affect information extraction from Brazilian legal documents.

===== PAPER: 10_02_Using deep learning to predict outcomes of legal appeals better than human experts =====
This paper addresses the problem of legal judgment prediction in Brazilian courts by training deep learning architectures (ULMFiT, BERT, and Big Bird) on 612,961 Federal Small Claims Courts appeals to predict their outcomes. The authors demonstrate that all models outperform 22 highly skilled human experts, with the best model (ULMFiT/AWD-LSTM) achieving a Matthews Correlation Coefficient of 0.3688 compared to 0.1253 from experts. The study also releases the BrCAD-5 dataset containing 765,602 appeals to promote further research in legal judgment prediction within the Brazilian judiciary. This work is directly relevant to legal NLP as it demonstrates the viability of transformer-based and recurrent architectures for processing long Brazilian legal texts and establishes benchmarks for the domain.

===== PAPER: 10_03_Artificial intelligence and speedy trial in the judiciary- Myth, reality or need? A case study in the Brazilian Supreme Court (STF) =====
This paper investigates the implications of artificial intelligence on speedy trial in the Brazilian Federal Supreme Court (STF), analyzing whether AI contributes to accelerating legal proceedings or merely provides ancillary benefits. Through a case study of the Victor system at the STF, which uses NLP to classify extraordinary appeals, the authors examine technical, negotiation, and result perspectives of AI adoption. The study develops a framework for understanding AI's impact on judicial speed, identifying both contributions and limitations including the unstructured nature of legal data and judges' individualistic writing styles. This work is relevant to legal NLP as it documents real-world deployment challenges of NLP systems in Brazilian courts and the importance of transforming unstructured legal data into structured information.

===== PAPER: 10_04_INACIA Integrating Large Language Models in Brazilian AuditCourts Opportunities and Challenges =====
This paper introduces INACIA, a system integrating Large Language Models into the Brazilian Federal Court of Accounts (TCU) for administrative case processing and assisted decision-making. INACIA uses pre-trained LLMs combined with search engines to extract information from case documents, reason through claims and evidence, and suggest adjudication directions to staff auditors while keeping humans in the decision loop. The authors propose a novel LLM-based evaluation methodology that correlates highly with human assessment, demonstrating moderate accuracy in capturing essential case elements. This work is highly relevant to legal NLP as it presents a production-ready pipeline for information extraction and reasoning from legal documents in Portuguese, addressing challenges of quality evaluation in the legal domain.
