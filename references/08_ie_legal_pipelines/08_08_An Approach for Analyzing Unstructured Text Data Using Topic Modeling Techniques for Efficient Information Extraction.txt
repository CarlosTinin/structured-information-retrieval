Abstract
Topic modeling techniques are popularly used for document clustering, large-scale
text analysis, information extraction from unstructured text documents, feature selection from large corpus, and various recommendation systems. This work suggested
a framework using topic modeling techniques for legal information extraction from
the Indian judicial system’s unstructured legal judgments. The suggested approach
aims to eliminate time-consuming manual judgment analysis in favor of automated
judgment analysis that can quickly examine large number of judgments in reduced
time span. In this work, we have experimented with different topic modeling methodologies for information extraction. The proposed framework is built on the Latent
Dirichlet Allocation, to categorize legal judgments into extracted topic groups.
Indian Supreme Court judgements are considered for the experimental setting. The
three main elements of the framework are pre-processing, applying the topic model,
and model evaluation using a coherence score metric. The framework was successfully applied to a corpus size of 100, 500, and 1000 legal judgments in batches. The
proposed framework is used to measure legal judgment similarity to demonstrate its
quantitative evaluation. In the future scope, various legal tasks that can benefit from
the proposed framework for performance improvement are suggested.

1 Background
1.1 Information Extraction from Unstructured data
The process of extracting meaningful information in a structured way from unstructured data in the form of entities, relations, objects, events, and many other forms is
known as information extraction (IE). This can be very useful to analyze huge amount
of unstructured text data in less time. The meaningful information extracted by IE is
used for semantic data analysis. Efficient and relevant information extraction always
improves the quality of data analysis. Nowadays, huge amount of unstructured data
exist online, in multiple forms such as images, running text, audio as well as video
clips, and time series data. All these different forms of data demand specialized techniques for IE and Data analysis. Peculiar characteristics of unstructured data are that it
is generated from diverse resources and it is schema-less. IBM predicted that unstructured data from diverse resources will compose 95% of total data available online by
2023. Due to the large volume and inherent characteristics of unstructured data, IE
becomes a challenging task to process unstructured data.
In today’s digital world, automated text analysis techniques are adapted to process
huge amounts of text data and get key insights from it. Natural Language Processing
(NLP) techniques which are derived from linguistics and statistics domain, along with
machine learning, are used for language automation. NLP tasks for text data processing include Automatic Text Summarization [1], Part-of-Speech Tagging [2], Sentiment
Analysis [3], Feature Extraction/Reduction [4], Relation/Event Extraction [5], and
Information Retrieval (IR) systems [6].
Information extraction methods are classified into three categories, i.e., rule based,
machine learning based and knowledge based. The rule-based approach is claimed to
be interpretable and suitable for rapid development and domain transfer [7], whereas
the machine learning approach for information extraction [8] gives high accuracy in
terms of precision and recall. But the machine learning approach is dependent on the
availability of the tagged dataset and the appropriate feature selection methodology followed. A knowledge base is an organized machine-readable data repository. Knowledge base population (KBP) systems [9] extract knowledge from available resources
and create knowledge bases based on the semantic and contextual information contained in the resources. The goal of a knowledge base population system is to automatically recognize entities from unstructured text documents, find facts about those
entities, and represent them in a structured knowledge base format. Information extraction automatically extracts relevant information for a particular application problem
from the available corpus and represents it in a machine-readable format. The objective
of information retrieval is to identify relevant information sources, whereas information extraction automatically extracts relevant information from identified sources in a
structured format.

1.2 Legal Information Extraction: Introduction and Motivation
IT, farming, manufacturing, and customer service are some of the industries
where artificial intelligence (AI) is having a growing impact. The Indian legal
sector has recently started gradually adapting AI for performance improvement.
National Judicial Data Grid (NJDG) reported that a huge number of cases are
pending in different courts in India which is reducing the effectiveness of the
Judiciary system and denial of justice to people. Legal Experts claimed that AI in
the legal sector will assist lawyers in conducting legal research quickly and effectively thus resolving the problem of significant pendency.
AI-enabled legal systems can tackle complicated problems by quickly locating
relevant cases and statutes. Such a system can highlight crucial points relevant
to specific contracts, after being “trained” on a large historical dataset to analyze
thousands of previous instances to create a legal knowledge base for reference.
A legal expert must always consult prior judgments rendered by various courts
when preparing a case file for a specific case in order to establish a solid case
basis. It takes a lot of time and efforts to manually sort through thousands of
declared judicial decisions to find the details that apply to the current case. There
are some reliable search engines accessible in the modern internet age to conduct
efficient legal searches. Speaking with legal professionals, we learn that the most
common criteria used to guide legal searches are things such as a specific act,
section, topics, facts, phrases, or keywords.
Different courts of the Indian Judiciary system deliver thousands of judgments
every month together. This large corpus of delivered judgments is an important
resource for the legal community in India. Lawyers, as well as Judges, frequently
refer to previous judgments for preparing case notes. Legal document analysis is
a time-consuming and difficult task for legal practitioners as well as the common
man due to the unstructured, lengthy, and complicated structure of legal documents. Automated legal document analysis can help legal practitioners and the
common man for a better understanding of the legal domain. IR and IE techniques
can be used for automatic processing and analysis of large number of unstructured legal documents like legal judgments.
AI can play a significant role for legal researchers and law firms by providing
legal intelligence for efficiency and transparency in the legal domain. Though AI
cannot replace the judges, it can be used as a facilitative tool to provide wider
access to justice for the common people. AI technology can be utilized in the
legal domain for the following legal tasks:

1.3 Representation Techniques for Text Documents
Text corpora modeling containing discrete data is a popular research problem. This
modeling finds data representation in the form of statistical relationships between
the words to facilitate basic text processing. The most common methodology used
by the IR community to model text documents is vectors. tf-idf [10] is a commonly
used technique, where a variable length document is represented as a fixed length
document-term matrix. the tf-idf score is calculated as the product of the frequency
count of each word in the whole corpus and how many times a word appears in
the individual document of a corpus. This score should be normalized for further
processing:

To overcome the shortcomings of the tf-idf scheme of sparse matrix representation, the IR research community invented a dimensionality reduction technique

text mining method in NLP to gain insights from text documents. It does not require
a tagged dataset for learning. The topic modeling algorithm is based on a dimensionality reduction technique fundamentally used for numerical data. NLP considers each word present in the corpus as a feature. Feature reduction using the topic
modeling technique will focus on the right content of text instead of considering
the whole text for processing. Topic modeling can process a large volume of text to
organize and understand a huge corpus of documents.

identify underlying topic structures—which are probability distributions in a set of
words—in the provided set of documents. The topic modeling mechanism’s primary
relevance is that it is unsupervised and does not require a labeled dataset.

3 Proposed Methodology
The objective of this study is to design an unsupervised information extraction
system to extract information from unstructured text data to generate knowledge
base. We identified the legal domain for information extraction using unstructured legal documents. Legal documents include legal contracts, law commission
reports, FIRs filed at various police stations, and legal judgments delivered by
the Indian judicial system. Some of these are semi-structured, while others are
completely unstructured. Judgments delivered by various courts are the most frequently searched resource by legal professionals among the available legal documents. These decisions are lengthy and completely unstructured. As a result,
applying efficient IR and IE techniques for rapid analysis of these documents is a
difficult task. As the availability of tagged legal datasets for court judgments from
the Indian Judicial system is a major concern, we focused specifically on unsupervised learning techniques for legal information extraction. This research aims
to use unsupervised learning techniques for Information Extraction from unstructured judgments.

aspects of the domain corpus. Distinguishing features of legal judgments are:

3.2 Data Preprocessing
A critical step in developing any NLP application is data cleaning and pre-processing of the corpus to be used. The quality of pre-processing can influence the efficiency of NLP system. The problem domain can decide which pre-processing techniques to use. Our pre-processing procedure follows the following steps.
a. Tokenization Tokenization is an essential step of the NLP pipeline which splits
raw text into words or sentences. We have performed word tokenization on the
text and removed all tokens having a length of fewer than three characters as short
words do not contribute much to the concepts. Token filtering using specific POS
tags is a common practice followed to process only relevant tokens further. We
have retained tokens having POS tags as one of the following {‘NOUN’, ‘ADJ’,
‘VERB’, ‘ADV’}
b. Lemmatization Lemmatization is a text normalization method used popularly in
NLP to convert each token to its root form. The goal of lemmatization is to reduce
inflectional derivationally related forms of a word to a common base form. We
have used a lemmatizer. lemmatize ()for lemmatization.

13

New Generation Computing (2024) 42:109–134

c. Stop word removal Stop words are the words in a running text which are filtered
to reduce text data volume for further processing. Legal documents have certain
terms occurring frequently and are not very conceptual for a legal document. We
have appended the stop word provided by NLTK for English with such legalspecific terms as {‘court’, ‘prosecution’, ‘case’, ‘appeal’, petitioner, petition,
respondent…} based on commonly occurring words across the legal judgments
and removed all stop words from the judgments.
d. Removal of Punctuation symbols We have removed all punctuation symbols from
the documents and converted the text into lowercase.
e. Removal of special symbols Removal of all special symbols from documents
makes them more interpretable for legal information extraction.
f. Phrase modeling using bi-gram and tri-gram A language model is used to predict
the probability of a sequence of words in a corpus. The N-gram model is used
to find frequently co-occurring words in the document. It assumes that words
occurring together in documents are semantically connected. Topic modeling
techniques use phrase modeling to identify similar context words in a text. Genism’s Phrases model is used to implement the bi-gram and tri-gram models from
the text.
g. Creation of Dictionary and Document-Term-Matrix LDA model takes Dictionary
and doc_term_matrix as two main parameters to run the model. A dictionary is
a mapping of the word to words in a corpus. To create doc_term_matrix, a bag
of words matrix representation is used where each document is represented as
a vector of tokens and the entries in this matrix represent the number of times a
word is found in a document.

indicates fewer topics in the document and a higher value implies a higher number
of topics in the mix.
3. Per topic word distribution denoted as ‘β’ the typical value β value is 0.1 or
200/N, where N is the number of words in the vocabulary of the corpus. At lower
values of ‘β’, the topics will have fewer words and at higher values of ‘β’ topics
will have more words
Hyper-parameter tunning is done for a wide range of topic sizes from 5 to 50 and
document corpus sizes of 100 to 1000 judgements. Each topic is having one unique
id, and set of words with their associated probability in the document collection as
shown in Fig. 5.

model can also visualize this topic mix and dominant topic in each document. Topic

Topic models are evaluated using a coherence score metrics which is a measure
of how good a topic model is in generating coherent topics ranging between 0 and
1. A higher coherence score indicates a better topic model. A coherent topic should
be semantically interpretable. The higher the value of the coherence score, better the
topic model is built. There is no single method for determining whether a coherence
score is good or bad. The score and its value are determined by the data from which
it is calculated. For example, a score of 0.5 may be adequate in one case but unacceptable in another. The objective of topic modeling technique is to maximize the
coherence score.
Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA) [40] and Non-negative Matrix Factorization
(NMF) [41] are the popular topic modeling techniques used for Information Extraction along with LDA. Topic modeling techniques such as LDA, LSA and NMF take
K = no. of topics to be retrieved as a hyper-parameter. At the outset, it is difficult to
predict the value of K, and generally, topic model needs to evaluated against different values of K to observe the coherence score and optimized value of K is labeled
as the one which gives maximum coherence score.

5 Conclusion and Future Scope
Our research goal is to build a knowledge foundation from the court decisions so
that the Indian legal community can have a legal knowledge base to refer. The biggest challenge involved for legal document analysis is the length and unstructured
format. The two most prominent techniques used for legal judgment representation
is tf-idf vector and doc2Vec representation. But both the techniques are having the
drawback of dealing with sparse vectors. BERT model is also popularly used for language modeling but not recommended for lengthy documents. Therefore, there is a
need for an efficient legal document representation technique for dealing with sparse
vectors.

5.1 Use of Topic Modeling Techniques for Legal Knowledge Graph Construction
We are recommending use of topic modeling techniques to extract legal information by focusing the important concepts of a judgment. Our long-term goal is to
organize the extracted legal information in the form of topics into a machine-readable structure like knowledge graph or concept graph. Each topic extracted from the
corpus can represent the individual concept of the judgment and linking between
different judgements based on topics extracted. Traditional entity relation extraction-based technique for constructing knowledge graph will have issues with scalability and accuracy of the constructed graph. Constructing legal knowledge graph
[43, 44] out of the topics extracted by our proposed approach can handle scalability
and give conceptual representation of the judgment. This machine-readable legal
knowledge graph can be helpful for a variety of legal applications, such as clustering
judgments, semantic legal search, Classification of Judgments, measuring judgment
similarity, and judgment summarization. The extracted topics are stored in lists for
time being but in future, we will try to construct legal knowledge graph comprising
of topics extracted from judgements as nodes and a vector representing the composition of a judgment topic wise. Though knowledge graph methods can be used for
legal domain, there are some inherent challenges involved:
• Meaning and representation of legal concepts are different for different coun-

tries.

• Constructing LegalAI knowledge graph is a complex process.
• Traditional knowledge graphs focus on relation between entities and LegalAI

demands legal concept explanation

Our future research directions are aiming at overcoming above challenges to construct legal knowledge graph for Indian Judiciary system.
Though unstructured text mining can be done effectively using Topic modeling
techniques, evaluating the quality of extracted topics is a big challenge involved with
it. Topic model evaluation is focusing on the ‘human interpretability’ or ‘semantic interpretability’ of the extracted topics. Evaluation of the topic model which

To extract legal information from our dataset, we used three different topic modeling techniques: LDA, LSA, and NMF. We found that LDA performed better in

legal applications. After applying the topic modeling technique to our dataset, we
can generate a labeled legal judgment corpus for the Indian judiciary system.
