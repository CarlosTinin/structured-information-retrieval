Judicial named entity recognition (JNER) is a basic task of judicial intelligence and judicial service informatization. At present, the research of JNER has attracted extensive attention. However, the existing JNER methods
usually can only assign a single label to a token in the input sequence, which is not applicable to nested entities
where a token may be assigned two or more diﬀerent labels at the same time. Therefore, this paper introduces the
machine reading comprehension (MRC) framework into JNER, and proposes a judicial nested NER method based
on the MRC. Firstly, we design the question template according to the characteristics of judicial nested named
entities, and construct the legal text named entity dataset in MRC format. Next, we introduce the span extraction
MRC model based on the pre-trained to encode the question and text, and learn the context knowledge of the
entity in the question. Finally, we extract the starting and end positions of the matching span respectively through
two classiﬁers, to get the corresponding entities. The experimental results on the information extraction dataset
in “CAIL2021” show, compared with the existing baseline models, the proposed method eﬀectively improves the
recognition eﬀect of nested entities commonly existing in the judicial ﬁeld.

1. Introduction
In recent years, with the opening of judicial big data and the development of artiﬁcial intelligence technology, “wisdom justice” has
gradually become a research hotspot, which can improve the eﬃciency
of case processing for judicial workers. Nowadays, wisdom justice services are roughly divided into three stages: (1) Assist judicial oﬃcers
to handle some simple, mechanical, and tedious tasks. (2) Learn artiﬁcial intelligence technology to assist in legal trials. (3) Active carry
out judicial-related convenience services in courts around the world,
such as intelligent judicial trials, and legal consultation (Zhong et al.,
2020; Kumar Sangaiah et al., 2020). In order to promote intelligent
technology-enabled and achieve a higher level of digital justice, China
Judicial Big Data Research Institute, Chinese Information Processing
Society of China, Tsinghua University, and other institutions have
launched the “Chinese AI & Law Challenge (CAIL)” for four consecutive
years since 2018. Further, the tasks of information extraction, similar
case retrieval and case label prediction have been launched recently in
CAIL 2021. The information extraction is a signiﬁcant research task under the wisdom justice services system, which is widely concerned in
industry and academia.

Named entity recognition (NER) task in information extraction is a
basic task in natural language processing, which aims to recognize the
required entities from unstructured text and marks them as predeﬁned
categories, such as Person, Location, Organization, etc. (Wang et al.,
2022; Li et al., 2022). In the legal text, NER’s main purpose is to extract
key information entities in the case, such as the criminal suspect, the
involved goods, the victim, the value of goods, etc. As a key and basic work in judicial artiﬁcial intelligence, judicial named entity recognition (JNER) has important application value in judicial judgment prediction (Zhong et al., 2020), judicial knowledge graph construction, similar
cases retrieval, and so on.
At present, AI has promoted the development of various ﬁelds and
the research of NER has made signiﬁcant progress in some general ﬁelds,
such as news, medical (Zhang et al., 2022; Luo et al., 2018) and ﬁnance.
However, the NER research in the judicial ﬁeld is relatively few. Compared with the general ﬁeld, legal texts are more complex and specialized, and often use nested named entities to describe detailed cases.
Thus, JNER is more diﬃcult. For example, the proportion of judicial
nested entities in CAIL2021 dataset is about 22%. In the theft case shown
in Fig. 1, the nested types of the entities include “Victim and Location”,

MRC task aims to understand the question and the relevant article to
answer questions automatically. As a general method, MRC has become
a paradigm for dealing with diﬀerent problems. Many basic technologies of natural language processing tasks, such as relation extraction,
event extraction and textual entailment, can achieve good results by
using reading comprehension methods. Inspired by these methods, we
converted NER tasks into MRC tasks. Each entity type is represented by
a related question query, and entities are extracted by answering these
queries in a given context. For example, extracting entities type with
the “victim” is formalized as extracting answer spans to the question
“Who is the victim in the case?”. By answering diﬀerent independent
questions, the problem of entity nesting is naturally solved.
In this paper, we employ the information extraction dataset in
CAIL2021 (CAIL-IE) as the experimental dataset and explore the method
of NER in the judicial ﬁeld. We transform the NER task into an MRC
task according to a span extraction-based MRC framework. We construct the question templates by introducing the entity’s label information and the legal prior knowledge that the entity implies. Then, combining with the proposed question, we use the span extraction MRC model
based on BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representation from Transformers) (Devlin & Chang, 2019) to extract the corresponding entities from
the case description context. In this way, we can extract two overlapping entities by answering diﬀerent categories of questions, and verify
the eﬀectiveness of the proposed method in CAIL-IE.

2. Related work
2.1. Named entity recognition (NER)
In the early research of NER, rule-based methods (Grishman & Sundheim, 1995; Black et al., 1998) play an important role. However, these
approaches usually require some domain experts to make rules manually, and this tedious manual design process is not only time-consuming
and labor-intensive, but also poorly transferable. Later on, machine
learning based methods have been used extensively for entity recognition tasks, such as decision tree method (DT) (Quinlan, 1986), maximum
entropy model (MEM) (Borthwick & Grishman, 1999), hidden markov
model (HMM) (Sun et al., 2002; Zhang et al., 2003; Jindal et al., 2020),
etc. The application of these methods has eﬀectively improved the NER
eﬀect. With the wide application of deep learning methods in natural
language processing, deep learning-based NER methods have gradually
become mainstream. This methods realize end-to-end entity recognition
through neural networks (Sharma et al., 2021) and achieve remarkable
results in general domains.
Collobert et al. (2011) ﬁrst proposed a multilayer neural network
structures to handle NER tasks, and Word2vec is used for word embedding to capture the semantics and context of words. Word2vec is a
word embedding model, proven to be eﬀective in representing word
meanings (Agrawal & Goyal, 2021). Lample et al. (2016), Ma and
Hovy (2016) added long short term memory (LSTM) (Hochreiter &
Schmidhuber, 1997; Rai et al., 2022) and convolutional neural networks (CNN) (Kim, 2014) modules respectively to the LSTM-CRF
model, to extract the character features of words, so that the model
can automatically learn character-level and word-level representations. Rei et al. (2016) proposed a combination method of wordlevel and character-level vectors based on the attention mechanism
to capture semantic information dynamically. In addition, there are
some researches on feature extraction through dilated convolution
neural networks and graph convolution neural networks (GCN), so
as to better model the dependence between characters and words.
Strubell et al. (2017) presented the Iterated Dilated Convolutional Neural Network model (IDCNN–CRF), which speeds up the training of the
model while capturing larger local features. Cetoli et al. (2017) used
a set of GCNs to study the role of dependency trees in NER. In recent years, the methods of NER by introducing lexicon information
have become an interesting research focus. For example, Zhang and

Yang (2018) presented a lattice-structured LSTM model, which avoids
the problem of error propagation caused by inaccurate Chinese word
segmentation, and explicitly utilizes word and word sequence information. Li et al. (2020) proposed a novel FLAT model for Chinese NER,
which has excellent parallelization ability and improves the recognition
eﬀect by introducing lexical information and well-designed position encoding. Aiming at the complex model structure with lexicon information, Ma et al. (2020) designed a simple and eﬀective method for incorporating word lexicons into the character representations for Chinese
NER. Liu et al. (2021) proposed a lexicon-enhanced BERT for Chinese
sequence labeling (LEBERT), and the incorporation of lexicon features
in the middle layer of BERT is achieved by designing a lexicon adapter.
There are also some methods to improve the recognition eﬀect of Chinese named entity by fusing multi-feature in the embedding layer of
the model. Li and Meng (2021) proposed a multi-feature fusion embedding method for Chinese NER by fusing semantic, glyph and phonetic
features together. Wu et al. (2021) proposed a multi-metadata embedding based cross transformer (MECT) to improve the performance of
Chinese NER by fuses the information of radicals, characters and words.
Liu et al. (2022) extracted the features of Chinese characters from three
aspects: character features, phonetic features and text features, and combined the multi-directional feature extraction of Chinese characters with
BiLSTM-CRF, and proposed a new named entity recognition method
(AIP).
In recent years, nested named entity recognition has gradually become a research focus of NER tasks, and methods such as graphbased networks and sequence labeling have been proposed successively.
Ju et al. (2018) proposed a Layered-BiLSTM-CRF model for dynamically
stacking ﬂat NER layers to recognize nested named entities. Each ﬂat
NER layer of the model uses bidirectional LSTM to capture sequential
context. The model combines the output of the LSTM layer in the current ﬂat NER layer to construct a new representation of the detected
entities. Seq2Seq model (Straková et al., 2019) regards nested NER as
a sequence-to-sequence problem, consisting of tokens input and labels
output sequence. The ﬁrst two methods based on sequence labeling have
the problem of error propagation between layers. What’s more, Luo and
Zhao (2021) proposed a new bipartite ﬂat graph network model BiFlaG for nested named entity recognition, which contains two modules,
namely, a ﬂat NER module for outermost entities and a graph module
for all inner entities.
There is also some research work speciﬁcally on the method of NER
in the judicial ﬁeld. Wang et al. (2020) proposed the JCWA-DLSTM
to recognize the entity of legal texts, which fuses multiple information
representations of characters, words and improves the eﬃciency of legal entity boundary recognition. Aiming at the lack of labeling data for
named entities in legal texts, Zhang et al. (2020) constructed a smallscale corpus and proposed a novel data augmentation method for JNER
by fusing multiple models. According to the long length of entity names
and the strong relevance between entities in judicial case documents,
Guo et al. (2022) proposed a NER method for legal documents, which
is based on the forward maximum matching strategy and community
attention mechanism.
In a word, we have made great breakthroughs in the research of
NER in the general ﬁeld. The model based on the deep neural network
has become the main NER solution. However, the existing solutions do
not fully consider the domain characteristics of Chinese judicial text,
and the nested entity recognition is mostly limited to the method of
sequence labeling. Therefore, it is particularly important to recognize
nested entities in the judicial ﬁeld.

After the public of this dataset, a series of reading comprehension models based on attention mechanisms have been proposed, such as BiDAF
(Bi-Directional Attention Flow) (Seo et al., 2017), R-NET (Wang et al.,
2017), QANet (Yu et al., 2018), etc. With the development of pre-trained
language models, MRC models based on BERT, RoBERTa (Liu et al.,
2019), and Albert (Lan et al., 2019) have emerged one after another,
driving the rapid development of MRC research.
In recent years, MRC frameworks have also been introduced in many
NLP tasks, such as relation extraction, event extraction, text classiﬁcation, semantic role labeling, textual entailment, sentiment analysis, etc.
For example, Li et al. (2019) transformed the task of relation extraction
into a Multi-Turn Question Answering task. Levy et al. (2017) simpliﬁed
the relationship extraction task without a pre-deﬁned relation category
to a reading comprehension task. Xiaoya Li et al. (2020) proposed a
uniﬁed framework for handling NER, which transforms the NER task as
an MRC task. Chen et al. (2020) proposed a Chinese event argument
extraction using the MRC framework, which describes argument roles
as natural language questions and achieves the recognition of argument
roles by answering diﬀerent questions in the context.
Based on the above work, the introduction of the MRC framework
can help solve the problem of multiple subtasks of information extraction and achieve good results. Thus, this paper introduces the MRC
framework into the NER, and proposes a judicial nested NER method
based on the MRC.
3. Method
Aiming at the problem of nested entities in judicial ﬁeld, we convert
the NER task into an MRC task by introducing an MRC framework. The
model structure is shown in Fig. 2, and it mainly includes two parts:
(1) Dataset Construction for MRC Framework: combining entity types
and judicial text data characteristics, we design the questions and mark
the answer positions from the given judicial context data. (2) Judicial
Nested Named Entity Recognition: the question and context are input
to the BERT MRC model, and the contextual knowledge of the entities
in the question is learned through encoding. We extract the spans of
answers from the context, and use them as the annotated entities. The
speciﬁc description is as follows:
3.1. Dataset construction for MRC framework
3.1.1. MRC questions construction
In the process of building a dataset oriented to reading comprehension, the questions will contain relevant knowledge information about
the entity and will have an impact on the ﬁnal result, so the generation
of MRC questions is the most important step. We analyze the CAIL-IE
dataset, which includes the plot description of the theft case, involving
10 types of related entities, namely “criminal suspect”, “victim”, “crime
tools”, “stolen goods”, “stolen currency”, “value of goods”, “theft profits”, “time”, “location”, “organization”.
Here, we design two diﬀerent question construction templates. Taking the entity type “crime tools” as an example, template 1 uses the
entity type as the question prompt, and the question is set to “What are
the crime tools in the case?”. Meanwhile, we analyze the legal texts of
the theft case and design question template 2 by combining the characteristics of judicially nested entities with the label descriptions deﬁned
by CAIL2021. Template 2 question is set to “What is the crime tool used
by the defendant in the case, which includes theft tools, vehicles, murder
weapons, etc.” The question query templates constructed for diﬀerent
entity types are shown in Table 1 in detail.

4.4. Experimental results and analysis
4.4.1. Entity recognition comparison
To verify the eﬀectiveness of the proposed model on nested entity
recognition, we conduct the following three groups of experiments:
(1) For comparison, we adopt BiLSTM-CRF, BERT-softmax, BERTBiLSTM-CRF and BERT-BiLSTM-IDCNN–CRF this four models as
baselines, and the speciﬁc experimental results are shown in
Table 4.
As illustrated in Table 4, compared with the BiLSTM-CRF model,
the P, R, and F1 of the remaining models have been greatly improved
after using the BERT pre-training model, which indicates that the pretraining model has strong text semantic representation ability. Using a
pre-training model can learn rich contextual semantic information and
further improves the eﬀect of entity recognition of judicial documents.
At the same time, compared with BERT-BILSTM-CRF, the R and F1 values of BERT-BILSTM-IDCNN–CRF are improved. It shows that although
BiLSTM model can extract global context features better, it will cause
local information loss, and the introduction of IDCNN can eﬀectively improve this shortcoming. In addition, the traditional sequence labeling
models may have incomplete entity recognition or wrong entity type
recognition when dealing with nested entities. However, the proportion of judicial nested entities in the experimental dataset is relatively
high, and our model can eﬀectively deal with nested entities. Therefore, as shown in Table 4, the eﬀect of our model is better than BERTBiLSTM-CRF, BERT-softmax and BERT-BiLSTM-IDCNN–CRF, three traditional sequence labeling models.

4.3. Comparison of experimental methods
In order to verify the performance of the method proposed in this
paper, the method is compared with the following seven methods on the
CAIL2021 information extraction dataset. These models are as follows:
(1) BiLSTM-CRF (Ma & Hovy, 2016) models are generic NER neural models, which use the context-free vectors as input and CRF
can consider the logical relationship between labels to obtain the
global optimal label sequence.
(2) BERT-BiLSTM-CRF model uses the BERT encoder to realize
context-aware representation based on BiLSTM-CRF.
(3) BERT-Softmax model in this paper is based on BERT provided by
CAIL 2021 information extraction task.
(4) BERT-BILSTM-IDCNN–CRF model adds IDCNN with a large acceptance domain for feature extraction, so that the model can
take into account both global and local features.
(5) Layered-BiLSTM-CRF (Ju et al., 2018) recognizes nested named
entities by dynamically stacking ﬂat NER layers.
(6) Seq2Seq model (Straková et al., 2019) regards nested NER as a
sequence-to-sequence problem, consisting of tokens input and labels output sequence.
