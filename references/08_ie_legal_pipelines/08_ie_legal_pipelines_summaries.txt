===== PAPER: 08_01_An entity-centric approach to manage court judgments based on Natural Language Processing =====
This paper presents an entity-centric infrastructure for managing court judgments through iterative NLP-based annotation combining machine learning and syntactic rule-based approaches, designed for sustainable development under scarce annotated data conditions. The framework supports multiple downstream tasks including faceted search and entity extraction (organizations, persons, locations, dates, monetary values), incorporating a human-in-the-loop approach aligned with organizational processes in Italian courts. The system is co-designed by scientific teams, institutional bodies, and implementation companies, with experiments demonstrating feasibility through various low-resource methods. This work is directly relevant to legal information extraction pipelines as it addresses the practical challenges of scaling NLP annotation systems for court documents at a national level.

===== PAPER: 08_02_Information extraction framework to build legislation network =====
This paper proposes an information extraction framework for building dynamic legislation networks from legal documents by identifying distinct expressions in legal text rather than using supervised learning approaches. The methodology applies approximate string matching techniques improved to produce reliable network datasets with more than 98% precision and recall for extracting citation relationships between legal documents. The resulting dynamic legislation network enables analysis of legislative complexity and inter-document relationships. This work is relevant to legal NLP as it demonstrates that rule-based information extraction with careful string matching can achieve high accuracy for structured legal references, complementing machine learning approaches for unstructured legal text.

===== PAPER: 08_03_A Frustratingly Easy Approach for Entity and Relation Extraction =====
This paper challenges the prevailing belief that joint models are necessary for end-to-end entity and relation extraction by presenting a simple pipelined approach using two independent BERT-based encoders that establishes new state-of-the-art results on ACE04, ACE05, and SciERC benchmarks with 1.7-2.8% absolute improvement in relation F1. The key insight is that entity and relation models benefit from learning distinct contextual representations, and sharing representations actually hurts performance, while fusing entity boundary and type information at the input layer of the relation model is crucial. The authors also propose an efficient approximation achieving 8-16x inference speedup with minimal accuracy loss. This work provides a strong baseline approach for legal information extraction pipelines that require both entity recognition and relation extraction from court documents.

===== PAPER: 08_04_A strategic framework for artificial intelligence in marketing =====
This paper develops a three-stage strategic framework for incorporating AI in marketing planning, distinguishing between mechanical AI (automating repetitive functions), thinking AI (processing data for decisions), and feeling AI (analyzing interactions and emotions). The framework is applied across marketing research, strategy (STP), and action stages, organized by the 4Ps/4Cs marketing framework. While comprehensive in its treatment of AI applications in marketing, this paper has limited direct relevance to legal NLP or information extraction from court decisions, as it addresses a fundamentally different domain and does not involve text processing or document analysis techniques.

===== PAPER: 08_05_A knowledge-centered framework for exploration and retrieval of legal documents =====
This paper presents CRIKE (CRIme Knowledge Extraction), a knowledge-based framework for legal knowledge extraction from court decisions built upon the LATO (Legal Abstract Term Ontology) ontology, which formalizes both conventional and abstract open-textured legal concepts. The framework employs a bootstrapping cycle of multi-label annotation for term extraction followed by information retrieval techniques for terminology enrichment, without requiring manual annotation by relying on existing conceptual knowledge. Experimental evaluation on 180,000 court decisions from the Illinois Caselaw Access Project demonstrates effective terminology discovery and retrieval improvement across bootstrapping cycles. This work is highly relevant to legal information extraction as it addresses the challenge of capturing abstract legal terminology that requires contextual interpretation, a core difficulty in processing criminal law decisions.

===== PAPER: 08_06_Judicial nested named entity recognition method with MRC framework =====
This paper addresses the problem of nested named entities in judicial texts, which traditional sequence labeling methods cannot effectively handle, by reformulating NER as a Machine Reading Comprehension (MRC) task where each entity type is represented by a question query. The approach constructs question templates incorporating entity label information and legal prior knowledge, then uses a BERT-based span extraction MRC model to extract corresponding entities from case descriptions. Experiments on the CAIL2021 information extraction dataset demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines for recognizing overlapping entity types such as "Victim and Location" or "Stolen Goods and Crime Tools." This work is directly relevant to legal NER pipelines where entities frequently nest within each other in criminal case descriptions, providing an effective solution for complex entity structures in judicial documents.

===== PAPER: 08_07_Enforcing legal information extraction through context-aware techniques- The ASKE approach =====
This paper presents ASKE (Automated System for Knowledge Extraction), a legal knowledge extraction approach combining context-aware embedding models with zero-shot learning in a three-phase cyclic extraction process (document chunk classification, terminology enrichment, and concept derivation) that operates without annotated data. The system projects document chunks and concepts into the same semantic space using Sentence-BERT, progressively extracting and organizing legal terminology into an ASKE Conceptual Graph across multiple generations. Evaluation on the EurLex dataset (45,000 EU legislative documents) shows ASKE outperforms BERTopic and achieves comparable results to Zero-Shot TM without requiring predefined topic counts, while qualitative evaluation on Italian case law demonstrates practical applicability. This work is highly relevant to legal information extraction as it addresses the fundamental challenge of operating in unlabeled legal corpora where fine-grained, chunk-level classification is needed.

===== PAPER: 08_08_An Approach for Analyzing Unstructured Text Data Using Topic Modeling Techniques for Efficient Information Extraction =====
This paper proposes a framework using topic modeling techniques, specifically Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), for automated information extraction from unstructured Indian Supreme Court judgments, eliminating the need for time-consuming manual analysis. The framework encompasses pre-processing, topic model application, and evaluation using coherence score metrics, tested on corpora of 100, 500, and 1000 legal judgments in batches. The approach enables legal judgment similarity measurement and categorization into extracted topic groups. This work is relevant to legal information extraction pipelines as it demonstrates an unsupervised approach to organizing and extracting information from large collections of court decisions, though its reliance on traditional topic modeling limits its semantic depth compared to transformer-based approaches.

===== PAPER: 08_09_CoLE- A collaborative legal expert prompting framework for large language models in law =====
This paper proposes CoLE (Collaborative Legal Experts), a prompting framework for enhancing LLM performance in legal tasks that simulates the collaborative division of labor among legal professionals, incorporating query intent identification, legal knowledge retrieval, and best demonstration retrieval modules. The framework addresses challenges of colloquial user queries, demonstration selection for in-context learning, and cost constraints by operating without annotated datasets or model parameter updates. Evaluation integrating CoLE with 7 general-purpose Chinese LLMs across 10 datasets shows it outperforms 8 specialized legal LLMs, with the Qwen-1.5-14B model improving from a score of 7 to 9 in expert evaluation. This work is relevant to legal NLP as it demonstrates that prompting frameworks with structured knowledge retrieval can enhance general-purpose LLMs to surpass fine-tuned legal models, offering a cost-effective alternative for legal information processing.
