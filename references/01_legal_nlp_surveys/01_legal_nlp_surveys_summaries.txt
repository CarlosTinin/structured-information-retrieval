===== PAPER: 01_01_Natural Language Processing for the Legal Domain A Survey =====
This paper presents a comprehensive survey of NLP applications within the legal domain, covering tasks such as legal question answering, judgment prediction, text classification, document summarization, named entity recognition, and argument mining. The authors systematically review datasets, language models, and methodologies tailored for legal text processing, noting that legal NER and corpora remain comparatively under-researched. The survey identifies key challenges including lengthy documents, complex legal language, fairness and bias concerns, and the need for domain-specific fine-tuning strategies. Published in ACM Computing Surveys (2025), this work provides a valuable reference for understanding the breadth of legal NLP research and future directions including multilingual capabilities and ontology-based reasoning.

===== PAPER: 01_02_A_Survey_on_Legal_Judgment_Prediction_Datasets_Metrics_Models_and_Challenges =====
This survey provides the first comprehensive overview of the Legal Judgment Prediction (LJP) task, cataloging 43 publicly available datasets across 9 languages, 16 evaluation metrics, and state-of-the-art models including multi-task, interpretable, and few-shot learning approaches. The authors propose a systematic classification of LJP subtasks based on legal systems and law domains, addressing a gap where prior surveys focused only on specific benchmarks like CAIL2018. Key challenges identified include the need for multilingual datasets, more realistic task formulations, fairness evaluation, and enhanced model interpretability. The work is relevant to legal NLP as it establishes a unified framework for comparing and advancing judgment prediction research across different jurisdictions.

===== PAPER: 01_03_Exploring_LLMs_Applications_in_Law_A_Literature_Review_on_Current_Legal_NLP_Approaches =====
This literature review examines the applications of Large Language Models (LLMs) in the legal domain, focusing on how transformer-based models are deployed across legal search, legal document review, and legal prediction tasks. The authors trace the evolution of NLP in law from early online search systems in the 1960s to modern LLM-based approaches, analyzing how models like BERT and GPT variants address challenges in contract review, judgment prediction, and information extraction. The paper highlights persistent challenges related to context length limitations, data availability, and interpretability in legal AI applications. This review is relevant as a broad mapping of how cutting-edge NLP technologies are being adapted for the specific demands of legal text processing.
