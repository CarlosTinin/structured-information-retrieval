Deep learning (DL) models achieved a lot of success due to the availability of labeled training data. In
contrast, labeling a huge amount of data by a human is a time-consuming and expensive solution. Active
Learning (AL) efficiently addresses the issue of labeled data collection at a low cost by picking the most
useful samples from a large number of unlabeled datasets. However, current AL techniques largely depend on
regular human involvement to annotate the most uncertain/informative samples in the collection. Therefore,
we introduce a Deep Learning Based Active Learning (DLBAL) method that may incrementally learn from a
small number of annotated training samples to build an effective classifier with optimum feature representation.
The proposed DLBAL method improves the contemporary AL approaches in two steps. First, EfficientNet-B0
networks are integrated into AL. The incrementally annotated useful samples may be used to update both the
representation of the feature and the classification algorithm concurrently. Second, we provide an effective
technique for sample selection that improves the performance of the classifier with fewer manual annotations
at a lower cost. In contrast to previous approaches that emphasize only uncertain instances with low prediction
confidence, we obtain a significant number of instances with high-confidence from the unannotated set for
pattern learning. Particularly, these instances are randomly picked and assigned labels to them continuously.
Detailed experiments are conducted to show that the proposed DLBAL system obtained reasonable results on
two difficult image classification datasets, namely Cross-Age Celebrity face recognition Dataset (CACD) and
Caltech-256.

the preceding step, and then Markov Random Field (MRF) is used
to enhance the classification performance of the model by enforcing
class label smoothness. However, the literature review concludes that
extensive human involvement in the AL technique results in increased

2022), and so on. The acquisition functions are a quick and simple
method for classifying datasets. The primary idea of AL is that if a
learning system is permitted to choose the training samples from which
it learns, it should perform more accurately with fewer labeled training
instances (Settles, 2009). As a result, this increases the significance of
the sample selection mechanism. In addition, the most used sample
selection technique is an uncertainty-based selection (Tong & Koller,
2001; Wang, Zhang, Li, Zhang, & Lin, 2016), which estimates the
uncertainty of unannotated data based on the predictions of earlier
classifiers, is one of the most prevalent techniques. Wang et al. (2016)
suggested that the sample with the greatest entropy on the conditional probability distribution of estimated labels be extracted as its
most uncertain sample. According to the SVM-based technique (Tong
& Koller, 2001), the distance between the most likely instances and
the decision border was used to select the unsure samples. Earlier
publications (Freund, Seung, Shamir, & Tishby, 1997; Kee, Del Castillo,
& Runger, 2018) also identified the sampling uncertainty relating to
a group of classification models (𝑖.𝑒. , the difference amid class labels
supplied by a group of classification models). In the literature, this
architecture is referred to as query-by-committee (Settles, 2009). All
of the aforementioned uncertainty-based approaches often disregard
the major portion of unlabeled data; therefore, they are susceptible
to outliers. In sample selecting, later approaches accounted for the
information density metrics and utilized the information of unlabeled
data. To reduce the prediction error of the trained classifier over unlabeled samples, these methods typically successively pick useful samples
based on probability estimates (Joshi, Porikli, & Papanikolopoulos,
2012; Wang, Sung, & Yau, 2011) or previous information (Kapoor,
Hua, Akbarzadeh, & Baker, 2009). For Instance, Joshi, Porikli, and
Papanikolopoulos (2009) evaluated the uncertainty-based sampling approach, which is effective in handling the multi-class scenario by using
the probability assessment of classes for each sample in the unlabeled
pool. In Kapoor et al. (2009), a priori context restrictions are included
to help users tag face photos more effectively. Simultaneously, it is suggested in several publications (Li & Guo, 2013; Zhao, Sun, Wang, & Cao,
2020) that samples must be taken in a way that maximizes the mutual
information that can be gained among the candidate sample and the
other unannotated samples when using the framework of the Gaussian
Process. Li and Guo (2013) introduced a unique adaptive AL technique
that labels crucial instances for image classifications by combining an
information density measure and the most uncertainty measure. In
addition, the variety of the picked sample within the given category has
been considered in Brinker (2003). This is the first research to extend
the SVM assisted AL method to batch mode. Elhamifar, Sapiro, Yang,
and Sasrty (2013a) have recently incorporated uncertainty assessment
across a single batching paradigm using convex scripting for selection
of unlabeled data. Such a technique is very compatible with all types of
classification models and is not restricted to those based on maximum
margin. All of the aforementioned AL approaches only evaluate lowconfidence data instances, ignoring the vast majority of high-confidence
data instances. However, it is believed that the more confidence data
instances will help to increase the classification accuracy and maintain
the consistency of classification models because of their prevalence and
stability. In addition, the employing of more confidence data instances
in our work will help to reduce manual annotation effort.

4.1. Dataset description

4.3. Implementation details

In this subsection, we assessed the proposed DLBAL framework
using two openly available benchmarks dataset, namely CACD (Chen
et al., 2015) and the Caltech-256 (Griffin et al., 2007). CACD is a sizable
demanding dataset for facial recognition and retrieval across ages. It
includes about 160,000 pictures of 2000 famous people, each with a
different age, posing, lighting, and occlusion. Due to the fact that not
all of the pictures are labeled, we use a subset of 256 people from the
entire dataset for our experiment, wherein 200 are initially labeled and
56 are labeled further by us. However, Caltech-256 is a demanding
dataset for object recognition including 30,607 real-world pictures of
varying sizes representing 257 classes (256 object classes and an extra
clutter class). At least 80 pictures are used to represent each class. This
dataset extends the Caltech-101 dataset. Fig. 4(a) and Fig. 4(b) show
various samples of the CACD and Caltech-256, respectively.

For implementation, we utilized the popular Deep Learning framework 𝑖.𝑒. , the keras-supported-tensorflow library was utilized within
the Python 3.7.1 programming environment. Existing models that have
been previously trained on considerable ImageNet datasets are used in
this experiment because we do classification. To improve the proposed
DLBAL method’s convergence, significant hyper-parameters have been
shifted. For the proposed method, different learning rates ranging from
0.1 to 0.0001 as well as dropout values between 0.3 and 0.50 have
been examined throughout the experiments, taking into consideration
the limited initial input data. In addition, we used Adam as well as the
SGD optimization algorithm for training the proposed DLBAL method.
However, the proposed system performed well employing the SGD
optimization algorithm during training. The proposed DLBAL method
takes an input size of 224 × 224 and a batch size of 32 while keeping
the number of epochs to 50 for both datasets during training and
testing. The proposed method on CACD and Caltech-256 dataset utilizes
a dropout of 0.50 between the last two Dense connected layers as
illustrated in Fig. 3. For a fair comparison with Contemporary methods,
the proposed model has been trained with a different no. of training
iterations as shown in Tables 1 and 2. The graphs in Fig. 5 depict the
desired performance obtained by these hyper-parameters.

4.2. Experimental setting
For the CACD dataset, all faces are resized to 224 × 224, and
the following parameters are set: 𝛿0 = 0.05, 𝑑𝑟 = 0.003 and 𝐾 =
5000. Whereas, for the Caltech-256 dataset, we scale every picture to
224 × 224 and set 𝛿0 to 0.05, 𝑑𝑟 to 0.003, and 𝐾 to 5000. Following
the parameters in the current AL technique (Elhamifar et al., 2013a),
we randomly choose 80% of pictures from every class to create the
unannotated training set, while the remaining pictures serve as the
testing set in our research. We randomly choose 10% of samples from
each class using the unlabeled training set to initialize the DLBAL
network, leaving the remaining samples for iterative learning. In addition, we use the same network architecture for the experiments of
the Caltech-256 and CACD datasets as shown in Fig. 3. Following
the configuration of Donahue et al. (2014), we continue by using
the pre-trained ImageNet ILSVRC dataset (Russakovsky et al., 2015)
model with the EfficientNet-B0 architecture for Caltech-256. Next, the
last layer is modified to carry out the Caltech-256 experiments while
keeping the other layers fixed. The learning rates are adjusted to 0.01
and 0.001 for CACD and Caltech-256, respectively. Furthermore, The
DLBAL method was implemented on the Python 3.7.1 platform using the Keras-supported-tensorflow framework. The experimental trials
were performed on a personal computer having Windows 10, a GeForce
RTX-3070 GPU from Nvidia, 32 GB of RAM, and the CUDA toolkit
11.0 with cuDNN v8.0. The results of the experiments demonstrated
the effectiveness of the proposed method, and a notable advancement
over contemporary methods.

4.4.3. Component analysis
The proposed DLBAL continuously feeds instances from the unannotated set to the Deep CNN Model and picks two types of instances
for fine-tuning, based on the prediction of the Deep CNN Model. The
first type includes the most uncertain minority samples, which have
low confidence in the prediction. While the second type consists of the
most certain majority samples having high-confidence in the prediction.
In addition, the proposed DLBAL takes into account three standard
active learning approaches for the selecting these unsure instances (𝓁𝑐𝑖 ,
𝑚𝑐𝑖 , and 𝑒𝑛𝑖 ). Whereas, the system provides pseudo-labels to these
certain majority samples automatically, without human intervention.
These two types of sample selection show different scores of confidence of the Deep CNN Model for the unlabeled dataset. Moreover,
to verify that the suggested DLBAL can consistently meet the common
relevant data samples selection requirements, we evaluate uncertain

Nowadays, DL algorithms have been showing reasonable performance in a variety of industrial applications because of the massive
amount of video data generated every day (Amin, Kim, Sami, Park,
& Seo, 2023). However, gathering annotated data on such a massive scale requires a lot of time and money, consequently making it
impossible to deploy DL models on many industrial systems. Therefore, AL has been concentrating on automating the procedure of data
annotation, although they often rely on humans for annotating ambiguous samples. In contrast to previous approaches that emphasize
only uncertain instances with low prediction confidence, the proposed
DLBAL framework gained a significant number of instances with higher
confidence from the unannotated set for pattern learning. Particularly,
these instances were randomly picked and assigned labels to them
continuously. Besides that, the high-confidence samples increased the
classification accuracy, reduced user annotation effort, and maintained
the stability of the classifier. Lastly, the average error rate at the
first iteration of the training process is relatively low, around 1.3%
and 5.5% on Caltech-256 and CACD datasets respectively. As a result,
the suggested DLBAL method can assign accurate pseudo-labels to
unannotated data while maintaining a reasonable error rate. Detailed
experiments are conducted to show that the proposed DLBAL system
obtained reasonable performance on two difficult image classification
datasets (CACD and Caltech-256). The proposed method performs well
than existing techniques but we believe that the classification accuracy
of the proposed method can be further improved. In the future, we
want to employ the Attention mechanism in our method and integrate
more celebrity data from the CACD dataset to improve the classification
accuracy.
