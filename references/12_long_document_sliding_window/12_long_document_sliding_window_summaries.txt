===== PAPER: 12_01_An Empirical Survey on Long Document Summarization Datasets, Models, and Metrics =====
This survey provides a comprehensive overview of long document summarization research, systematically analyzing benchmark datasets, summarization models, and evaluation metrics as the three key components of the research setting. The authors highlight that long documents require extra compression through identification of key narratives scattered across the source, and that existing evaluation metrics like ROUGE do not correlate well with human quality assessments for long documents. Through ad-hoc experiments, they systematically investigate architectures and mechanisms including extractive, abstractive, and hybrid approaches applied across various domains. This work is relevant to legal NLP as it establishes the theoretical and practical foundations for processing long legal documents that exceed standard transformer input limits, a challenge directly encountered when applying NER to lengthy judicial decisions.

===== PAPER: 12_02_Efficient Long-Text Understanding with Short-Text Models =====
This paper proposes SLED (SLiding-Encoder and Decoder), a method for processing long sequences by partitioning input into overlapping chunks, encoding each with a pretrained short-text encoder, and using a pretrained decoder to fuse information across chunks via fusion-in-decoder. SLED achieves competitive performance with specialized models up to 50x larger on the SCROLLS benchmark spanning seven long-range language understanding tasks, without requiring expensive pretraining from scratch. The key assumption is that input tokens can be contextualized through local surroundings while global cross-chunk reasoning is handled by the decoder. This work is directly relevant to legal NLP as it provides a practical sliding window strategy for applying standard pretrained models to long legal documents, analogous to the sliding window approach used for NER on lengthy judicial sentences.

===== PAPER: 12_03_Efficient Memory-Enhanced Transformer for Long-Document Summarization in Low-Resource Regimes =====
This paper presents EMMA, the first memory-enhanced encoder-decoder transformer for long-document summarization, addressing the challenge of quadratic memory growth in self-attention when processing lengthy inputs. The architecture combines a segmentation algorithm that splits documents into chunks paired with related target summary parts, and a recursive memory module that stores information from previously processed chunks. EMMA achieves competitive results with state-of-the-art models under full training conditions and outperforms prior works in few-shot learning, while maintaining constant GPU requirements regardless of document length. This work is relevant to legal NLP as it demonstrates that chunk-based processing with cross-chunk memory can effectively handle long documents in low-resource settings, directly applicable to processing lengthy legal decisions where computational resources are limited.
