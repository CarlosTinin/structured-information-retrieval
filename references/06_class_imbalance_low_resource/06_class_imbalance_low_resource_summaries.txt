===== PAPER: 06_01_Two-stage fine-tuning with ChatGPT data augmentation for learning class-imbalanced data =====
This paper addresses the challenge of class-imbalanced text classification when fine-tuning large pre-trained language models, which tend to overfit minority classes due to limited representation. The authors propose a two-stage fine-tuning strategy where Stage 1 pre-fine-tunes only the final layer using ChatGPT-generated class-balanced augmented data to provide minority classes a head start, followed by standard fine-tuning in Stage 2. The approach outperforms vanilla fine-tuning, LDAM, and class-balanced loss methods on imbalanced benchmarks and a real-world ADME semantic labeling task, demonstrating superior out-of-distribution generalization. This work is relevant to legal NLP pipelines where court decision classification frequently exhibits severe class imbalance across decision types.

===== PAPER: 06_02_Pre‑trained transformer‑based language models for Sundanese =====
This paper addresses the lack of NLP resources for Sundanese, a low-resource language with over 32 million speakers, by pre-training three monolingual Transformer-based language models (GPT-2, BERT, and RoBERTa) on limited Sundanese data. When evaluated on downstream emotion classification of Sundanese tweets, the monolingual RoBERTa model significantly outperformed larger multilingual models despite smaller pre-training data, while BERT performed slightly better than XLM-RoBERTa. The authors additionally assess social bias in their models, finding neutral behavior, and release all models publicly on HuggingFace. This work provides evidence that monolingual pre-training on limited domain-specific corpora can outperform multilingual alternatives, a finding directly applicable to legal NLP in under-resourced languages such as Portuguese legal text.

===== PAPER: 06_03_Pre-Trained Transformer-Based Models for Text Classification Using Low-Resourced Ewe Language =====
This paper tackles the absence of NLP resources for the Ewe language, an extremely low-resourced African language with complex structure and no publicly available datasets, by developing a custom dataset and word embeddings for text classification. The authors fine-tune seven pre-trained transformer models and find that BERT-based architectures are superior at comprehending Ewe text semantics, while DeBERTa demonstrates capability in capturing long-range dependencies. The study contributes baseline results and publicly available resources for Ewe language processing. This work is relevant to the broader challenge of applying transformer models to low-resource language scenarios, paralleling challenges faced when processing legal documents in underrepresented languages.

===== PAPER: 06_04_An Integrated Topic Modelling and Graph Neural Networkfor Improving Cross-lingual Text Classification =====
This paper addresses the challenge of cross-lingual text classification (CLC) where labeled data in target languages is scarce, proposing TG-CTC, a model that integrates neural topic modeling with heterogeneous text graph representation learning via Graph Attention Networks (GAT). The approach fuses latent topic embeddings produced through variational auto-encoding with contextual representations from multilingual pre-trained models (XLM-RoBERTa), then applies multi-layered GAT to capture long-range structural relationships in cross-linguistic document graphs. Experiments on Amazon Reviews, XGLUE News, and Facebook Multilingual SLU datasets demonstrate effectiveness over state-of-the-art CLC techniques. This work is relevant to legal NLP scenarios involving multilingual legal corpora where annotated resources in target languages are unavailable.

===== PAPER: 06_05_From_Pre-Training_to_Meta-Learning_A_Journey_in_Low-Resource-Language_Representation_Learning =====
This paper presents a comprehensive exploration of low-resource language representation learning for Greek, addressing tasks including sentiment analysis, irony detection, hate speech detection, and emotion classification. The authors develop a Greek Longformer model for processing longer texts beyond 512 tokens and propose an ensemble meta-learning framework combining Greek BERT (512 tokens), Greek BERT with rolling predictions, and the Greek Longformer. Domain adaptation through continued pre-training on Greek media data and contrastive learning techniques yield improved performance across all classification tasks. This pipeline of pre-training, domain adaptation, and meta-learning ensemble strategies is directly applicable to legal NLP in low-resource settings where document lengths often exceed standard transformer limits.

===== PAPER: 06_06_Enhancing Indian legal judgment classification with embeddings =====
This paper introduces a method for classifying Indian court judgments into 14 legal domains using diverse embeddings from models including InLegalBERT, T5, RoBERTa, and others combined with classical machine learning classifiers. The authors employ SMOTE to address class imbalance, PCA and forward feature selection for dimensionality reduction, and voting classifier ensembles, achieving 98% accuracy with the T5+SMOTE+feature selection+voting classifier configuration. The work addresses the unique complexity of India's hybrid legal system combining common, civil, and customary law traditions with multilingual documentation. This study is highly relevant to legal document classification pipelines, demonstrating that domain-specific BERT embeddings combined with class imbalance techniques can achieve strong performance on court decision categorization tasks.
