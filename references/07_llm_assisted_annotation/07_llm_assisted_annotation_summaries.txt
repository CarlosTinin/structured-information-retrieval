===== PAPER: 07_02_ChatGPT_Label_Comparing_the_Quality_of_Human-Generated_and_LLM-Generated_Annotations_in_Low-Resource_Language_NLP_Tasks =====
This paper systematically compares the quality of LLM-generated annotations (using ChatGPT) against human-generated annotations for NLP tasks in Turkish, Indonesian, and Minangkabau, three languages with varying resource availability. The study addresses the growing reliance on LLMs for data annotation while questioning their reliability across different task complexities and language contexts, particularly for low-resource languages. The authors evaluate annotation quality through metrics and comparative examples, examining when LLM-generated annotations can confidently replace human expertise. This work is relevant to legal NLP pipelines operating in low-resource language contexts where obtaining expert human annotations for legal documents is expensive and time-consuming.

===== PAPER: 07_03_Open-source LLMs for text annotation- a practical guide for model setting and fine-tuning =====
This paper evaluates open-source LLMs (LLaMA, FLAN) for text annotation tasks typical in political science research, comparing zero-shot, few-shot, and fine-tuning approaches against GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and crowd-workers across eleven classification tasks. The key finding is that fine-tuning substantially improves open-source LLM performance, allowing them to match or surpass zero-shot GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, and that fine-tuning with 250-500 annotated examples is preferable to few-shot learning. The authors provide practical recommendations including running LLMs at low temperature for consistency and always validating with human experts. This work provides directly applicable guidance for researchers using LLMs to annotate legal documents, supporting the case for fine-tuned open-source models as cost-effective, reproducible, and data-protected alternatives to proprietary APIs.

===== PAPER: 07_04_Using ChatGPT to annotate a dataset- A case study in intelligent tutoring systems =====
This paper presents a case study on using ChatGPT for dataset annotation in the context of intelligent tutoring systems, specifically for labeling student states with recommended pedagogical actions. The authors employ prompt engineering to generate action lists, manually filter them, then instruct ChatGPT to assign appropriate actions to student states, followed by manual analysis of incorrect labels. The study demonstrates that while ChatGPT-based annotation shows promising results approaching human performance, careful manual verification remains necessary. This work is relevant to legal NLP as it provides methodology for using LLMs as annotation tools in domain-specific contexts where expert labeling is expensive, analogous to the challenge of annotating legal documents for classification and NER tasks.

===== PAPER: 07_05_An Entity Extraction Pipeline for Medical Text Records Using Large Language Models- Analytical Study =====
This paper introduces a modular LLM pipeline for semantically extracting clinical features from unstructured patient admission records, addressing security concerns and hallucination issues inherent in LLM applications. The pipeline processes concept extraction, aggregation, question generation, corpus extraction, and Q&A scale extraction using local low-parameter LLMs (Qwen-14B-Chat and Baichuan2-13B-Chat), achieving over 95% accuracy and precision on 25,709 pregnancy cases. Notably, an INT4-quantified version delivers enhanced performance (97.28% accuracy) on consumer-grade hardware, demonstrating practical deployability. This work is highly relevant to legal information extraction pipelines as it demonstrates a modular, privacy-preserving approach to structured data extraction from unstructured text using locally deployed LLMs.

===== PAPER: 07_06_Sample Size Considerations for Fine-Tuning Large Language Models for Named Entity Recognition Tasks- Methodological Study =====
This paper addresses the lack of actionable guidance on training sample sizes for fine-tuning LLMs for NER tasks in specialized domains, using conflicts of interest disclosure statements as the experimental domain. The authors fine-tune BERT and GPT model variants on 2500 stratified random samples of varying sizes and find that relatively modest samples (439-527 sentences) reach a point of diminishing returns, with entity density (entities per sentence) being an equally important predictor of model performance. Results show fine-tuned models achieving F1-scores of 0.79-0.96, with training data quality and architecture choice being as important as data volume and model size. This study provides critical practical guidance for legal NER applications where gold-standard annotation is expensive, demonstrating that small expert-annotated datasets can yield substantial improvements.

===== PAPER: 07_07_Two-stage fine-tuning with ChatGPT data augmentation for learning class-imbalanced data =====
This paper addresses the challenge of class-imbalanced text classification when fine-tuning large pre-trained language models, proposing a two-stage strategy where Stage 1 pre-fine-tunes the final layer with ChatGPT-generated balanced augmented data, followed by standard fine-tuning in Stage 2. The approach outperforms vanilla fine-tuning, LDAM, and class-balanced loss methods on imbalanced benchmarks and demonstrates superior out-of-distribution generalization when using ChatGPT for data augmentation. The authors show that providing minority classes a head start through balanced pre-fine-tuning effectively mitigates the bias toward majority classes inherent in standard fine-tuning. This work is relevant to legal document classification where decision categories (e.g., conviction, acquittal, extinction) are inherently imbalanced.
