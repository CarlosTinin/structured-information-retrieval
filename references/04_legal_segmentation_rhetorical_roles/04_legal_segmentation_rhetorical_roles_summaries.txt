===== PAPER: 04_01_LAWSUIT a LArge expert-Written SUmmarization dataset of ITalian constitutional court verdicts =====
This paper introduces LAWSUIT, the first large-scale Italian legal abstractive summarization dataset comprising 14,000 verdicts from the Constitutional Court of the Italian Republic with expert-authored summaries (maxims). The dataset features uniquely challenging characteristics including lengthy source texts (average 4,688 words), uniformly distributed salient content rather than concentrated in specific sections, and high-quality targets authored by legal experts. Experiments with sequence-to-sequence and segmentation-based approaches reveal that segmentation-based pipelines achieve superior results in both full and few-shot settings. This work is relevant to legal document segmentation as it demonstrates how hierarchical document structure and segment-based processing improve summarization of lengthy legal texts in non-English jurisdictions.

===== PAPER: 04_02_DeepRhole deep learning for rhetorical role labeling of sentences in legal case documents =====
This paper proposes deep learning models for automatically identifying rhetorical roles (Fact, Argument, Ratio, Final Judgement, etc.) of sentences in legal case documents, substantially outperforming prior CRF-based methods that relied on hand-crafted features. The authors evaluate hierarchical BiLSTM, BiLSTM-CRF with attention, and transformer-based models (BERT, LegalBERT) across five law domains and two jurisdictions (Indian and UK Supreme Courts), achieving Macro F-scores of 0.821 and 0.600 respectively. An extensive inter-annotator study reveals that certain rhetorical label pairs are inherently subjective, with confusion patterns among human annotators mirrored in model predictions. This work is foundational for legal document segmentation as it demonstrates that deep learning can generalize rhetorical role identification across domains and jurisdictions without expensive hand-crafted features.

===== PAPER: 04_03_CoTHSSum- Structured long-document summarization via chain-of-thought reasoning and hierarchical segmentation =====
This paper proposes a novel summarization framework that integrates hierarchical input segmentation with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting to guide LLMs through structured reasoning for long-document summarization. The method decomposes documents into semantically coherent segments, applies CoT-based prompting for intermediate summary reasoning, and employs structure-guided decoding for final summary composition. Evaluated across five diverse datasets including the CAILsfzy legal dataset using Qwen, LLaMA, and Phi backbones, the approach consistently outperforms baselines on ROUGE, BLEU, BERTScore, and factual consistency metrics. This work is relevant to legal document segmentation as it demonstrates that hierarchical segmentation combined with structured reasoning significantly improves the processing of lengthy, complex legal texts.

===== PAPER: 04_04_Legal sentence boundary detection using hybrid deep learning and statistical models =====
This paper addresses sentence boundary detection (SBD) in legal texts, proposing a hybrid architecture that combines a CNN model with a CRF model to achieve a 4% F1-score improvement over baseline approaches. The authors evaluate various deep learning models including domain-specific transformers (LegalBERT, CaseLawBERT), identifying the CNN as the best-performing model when considering size, F1-score, and inference time together. The hybrid model also outperforms standard open-source SBD libraries on an out-of-domain Indian legal test set, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities. This work is relevant to legal document segmentation as accurate sentence boundary detection is an essential preprocessing step for downstream tasks including rhetorical role labeling, summarization, and information extraction from legal texts.
