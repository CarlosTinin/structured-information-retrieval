===== PAPER: 03_01_Design of Knowledge Graph Retrieval System for Legal andRegulatory Framework of Multilevel Latent Semantic Indexing =====
This paper designs a knowledge graph retrieval system for the Chinese legal domain, combining multilevel latent semantic indexing with a Neo4j graph database built on the Spring Boot framework. The authors analyze singular value distributions in latent semantic space using Zipf's law and propose a bi-LSTM-CRF model for named entity recognition in legal texts, which achieves significant improvements in crime prediction tasks. The system supports knowledge query, recommendation, and similar case retrieval for procuratorial affairs through entity-based knowledge graph construction. This work is relevant to legal NLP as it demonstrates how NER and knowledge graphs can be integrated to build intelligent judicial assistance systems that improve case-handling efficiency.

===== PAPER: 03_02_Adversarial training flat-lattice transformer for named entity recognition of chinese legal texts =====
This paper proposes AT-Flat, an Adversarial Training Flat-Lattice Transformer model for Named Entity Recognition in Chinese legal judgment documents, addressing the challenge of nested entities and professional terminology that blur entity boundaries. The model combines character and word information through the Flat-Lattice Transformer architecture while introducing adversarial training to integrate word boundary information from Chinese Word Segmentation, filtering noise to enhance NER performance. Experiments on Chinese traffic accident and financial lending judgment documents demonstrate that AT-Flat outperforms state-of-the-art methods on both domain-specific and public Chinese NER datasets. This work is significant as it is the first to apply adversarial training to handle nested NER in Chinese judicial documents, providing a practical solution for legal information extraction.

===== PAPER: 03_03_A domain-specific cross-lingual semantic alignment learning model for =====
This paper proposes CLWKD, a cross-lingual mapping and knowledge distillation framework for multi-granularity semantic alignment in low-resource language scenarios, with applications in legal and medical domains. The framework addresses three challenges: domain-specific data scarcity, semantic space heterogeneity between languages, and complex agglutinative morphology, by combining general-domain mapping matrices with domain-specific knowledge distillation across token, word, and sentence levels. The approach is particularly designed for languages like Mongolian that suffer from severe data scarcity and complex morphological structures. This work is relevant to legal NER as it provides methods for transferring NLP capabilities from high-resource languages to low-resource ones in specialized domains including law.

===== PAPER: 03_04_Fine-grained legal entity annotation- A case study on the Brazilian Supreme Court =====
This paper presents a case study on fine-grained nested named entity annotation of Brazilian Supreme Court (STF) decisions, producing the largest manually annotated Portuguese legal NER corpus with 594 decisions containing 4 coarse-grained and 24 fine-grained entity types annotated by 76 law students. The annotation methodology integrates teaching and research by conducting the task as part of coursework, using Cohen's Kappa for inter-annotator agreement measurement across three annotation phases. Baseline NER experiments using CRF and BiLSTM-CRF models achieve F1 scores exceeding 0.9 for most entity types, demonstrating the corpus's utility for training legal NER systems. Published in Information Processing and Management (2022), this work makes a significant methodological and substantive contribution to legal NLP in Portuguese by addressing the scarcity of annotated legal corpora for NER.

===== PAPER: 03_05_Few-shot Named Entity Recognition - Definition, Taxonomy and Research Directions =====
This survey provides a comprehensive review of few-shot Named Entity Recognition (FS-NER), proposing a taxonomy that divides approaches into model-centric methods (meta-learning, transfer learning, prompt-based methods) and data-centric methods (active learning, distant supervision, data augmentation). The authors formally define the FS-NER problem and systematically analyze algorithms in chronological order within each subcategory, highlighting how later techniques address limitations of earlier methods. A benchmark study of seven well-known few-shot learning methods for NER is also provided to facilitate comparison. This work is relevant to legal NER applications where annotated data is scarce and expensive to produce, offering practitioners guidance on selecting appropriate low-resource NER techniques.

===== PAPER: 03_06_A novel large-language-model-driven framework for named entity recognition =====
This paper proposes LLMCC, a novel NER framework that synergistically combines decoder-based LLMs (like GPT-4) with encoder-based LLMs (like DeBERTa-v2) through in-context learning, cross-model interactions, and entity-aware contrastive learning. Key innovations include SemnRank, a demonstration retriever that addresses redundancy through semantic similarity and bidirectional entailment detection, and InforLaw-thought, a prompting method based on information laws (Zipf's law, Heaps' law) to improve text comprehension. Experiments across five domains demonstrate that LLMCC surpasses recent state-of-the-art NER methods. This work is relevant to legal NER as it establishes a paradigm for leveraging complementary strengths of different LLM architectures in the era of large language models, applicable to domain-specific entity extraction tasks.

===== PAPER: 03_07_Legal Entity Extraction- An Experimental Study of NER Approach for Legal Documents =====
This paper presents an experimental study on NER for legal documents, including an extensive review of manual annotation tools, the creation of a legal NER corpus of 5,000 judgment papers with 8 legal named entity types, and the development of an entity-relation system based on spaCy's pre-trained model. The authors define domain-specific entities (legal terms, acts, legal institutes) alongside general entities (person, location, date) and evaluate annotation tool requirements for legal corpus construction. The work contributes to legal NLP by providing practical guidance on building NER training corpora for the legal domain and demonstrating the application of pre-trained NER models to legal judgment papers.

===== PAPER: 03_08_An entity-centric approach to manage court judgments based on Natural Language Processing =====
This paper presents an entity-centric infrastructure for managing Italian court judgments, combining machine learning and syntactic rule-based NLP services for iterative document annotation in a sustainable, scalable framework. The system addresses the challenge of low-resource annotation data through complementary approaches and incorporates a human-in-the-loop methodology aligned with organizational processes in Italian courts. The design results from cooperative co-design between scientific teams, institutional bodies, and implementation companies, with experiments demonstrating feasibility while discussing challenges for national-level scaling. This work is relevant to legal NER as it proposes a practical, production-oriented framework for entity extraction from court documents that accounts for real-world constraints of limited annotated data and institutional requirements.

===== PAPER: 03_09_Combining prompt-based language models and weak supervision for labeling named entity recognition on legal documents =====
This paper investigates alternative labeling strategies for legal NER by combining GPT-3 prompt-based generation and weak supervision as substitutes for expensive human annotation on Brazilian Official Gazette documents. Results show that while human labeling maintains the best performance, weak supervision preserves 95.6% of human-trained model scores, GPT-3 achieves 74.0%, and their combination reaches 90.7%, all at significantly lower annotation costs. The authors also demonstrate that combining GPT-3 with just 30% human labeling achieves 83.9% of full human performance. This work is highly relevant to legal NER as it provides evidence-based strategies for reducing annotation costs in legal document processing while maintaining acceptable model performance.

===== PAPER: 03_10_An experimental study on data augmentation techniques for named entity recognition on low-resource domains =====
This paper systematically evaluates the effectiveness of two data augmentation techniques—Mention Replacement and Contextual Word Replacement—on NER models (Bi-LSTM+CRF and BERT) across three low-resource domain datasets including legal texts. The authors demonstrate that augmentation is particularly beneficial for smaller datasets but identify a saturation point beyond which additional augmentation degrades model quality, with no universally optimal number of augmented examples. Key findings show that Contextual Word Replacement generally outperforms Mention Replacement, and BERT models benefit more from augmentation than Bi-LSTM+CRF architectures. This work provides practical guidance for legal NER practitioners working with limited annotated data, emphasizing the need for careful experimentation with augmentation quantities.
