Dimensions corresponding to large singular values in the
latent semantic space represent more “commonality” and
less “personality” of language elements, while dimensions
corresponding to small singular values represent more
“personality” and less “personality” of language elements.
From this, the approximate and implicit correspondence
between the dimension of the latent semantic space and the
conceptual granularity is obtained, which provides a new
idea for understanding and utilizing the dimension of the
latent semantic space. By using ontology technology and
Neo4j graph database to store data and building a knowledge
graph system in the field of public prosecutorial affairs on
this data, functions such as knowledge query, knowledge
recommendation, and knowledge management are realized.
Implementing online import, natural language query, and
similar case push for entity nodes and relationships not only
diversifies search results, but also enriches knowledge
connections and ensures the practicability of knowledge
graphs in the public domain of procuratorial affairs. In this
paper, CRF is added to bi-LSTM to optimize named entity
recognition, that is, the bi-LSTM-CRF model. It considers
both contextual information and the correlation between
labels. We use word2vec to train word vectors, only use
entity labeling, that is, character-level labeling, and use BIOE
mode to train the model. The text representation of the legal
text, the word vector representation of the legal text, and the
related knowledge entity vector representation are taken as
multichannel inputs, and the network can learn a more
adequate text representation by fusing the information from
multiple channels. The method proposed in this paper has
achieved significant improvement in the task of crime
prediction in different types of cases. The core functions of
the system include basic information inquiry of relevant
cases, basic information inquiry of case suspects, entity
inquiry, push of similar sentencing cases, evidence review,
and other functions. This paper verifies the accuracy and
feasibility of the system.

--- ABSTRACT ---
Judgment documents are the legally binding written conclusion made by the court
based on the facts of the case and the law. Due to the use of professional terms and
nested combinations of words, potential information of judgment documents has
not been deeply excavated. Named Entity Recognition (NER) is a necessary task in
Natural Language Processing (NLP), and has been widely introduced into Chinese
texts processing for many years. However, the professional terms and nested words
lead to the boundaries between entities being blurred, which can not accurately
divide entities. In this paper, a new NER model Adversarial Training Flat-Lattice
Transformer (AT-Flat) which combines adversarial training and Flat-Lattice Transformer (Flat) is proposed to weaken these problems. In AT-Flat, the Flat combines
character and word information to get sequence information, and the CRF is used
to output the final entity prediction results. Moreover, the key point is an adversarial
training framework introduced to integrate task-shared word boundary information from Chinese Word Segmentation (CWS) task into Chinese NER task. The
framework is able to filter out the noise caused by CWS task and further enhance
the effect of Chinese NER task. More importantly, experiments on NER task of
Chinese traffic accident and financial lending judgment documents show that our
method outperforms other state-of-the-art methods. These verify that our method
can effectively alleviate the problem of poor NER effect caused by professional
terms and nested words. In addition, three public Chinese NER datasets were also
used to evaluate our method.

--- INTRODUCTION ---
The amount of data in the judicial field has increased rapidly in recent years. Legal
professionals such as judges, lawyers, and prosecutors not only handle a large number of cases, but also need to consult numerous references. Judgment documents are
the legally binding written conclusion made by the court based on the facts of the
case and the law. With the development of judicial online system, nearly 100 million
judgment documents have been published online in China, accumulating abundant
judicial data of various cases (Tang et al. 2020). Judgment documents can help professionals get similar cases to assist in sentencing. The information mining of judgment documents can reduce the labor cost of professionals and improve legal work
efficiency.
Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a common way for processing data, of
which Named Entity Recognition (NER) is an indispensable research topic. NER
aims to extract the words or expressions denoting specific entities from documents
(Huang et al. 2020), which is composed of two parts: detecting the entity boundaries
and classifying the entities into predefined categories such as Name, Judicial Organization, and Location (Tang et al. 2020). Chinese judicial NER can improve the
efficiency of court operations and provide support to judges in their trial work.
Due to the differences from other languages such as English, there are significant
challenges in the Chinese NER task. The reasons are as follows: first, the Chinese
language doesn’t have spaces between words and lacks semantic features like uppercase, which makes many NER methods are not suitable for Chinese; secondly, the
Chinese dataset for NLP is small compared to the English dataset; third, there are
multiple meanings of words in Chinese, and the meaning of the same word may be
vary in different contexts (Chang et al. 2021).
Furthermore, judicial entities are mostly nested, making structures complicated.
For example, the nested types of the legal entities in Fig. 1, include "Judicial Organization and Location", "Causing-trouble Vehicle and defendant", "Location and Defendant", and “Stolen Goods and Causing-trouble Vehicle". "

in other text (Tang et al. 2020). On the one hand, it leads to a decline in the accuracy of entity recognition. On the other hand, it loses the correlation between words,
which also affects the subsequent downstream tasks (Zhang et al. 2023). These entities are rare in other domains, which makes some existing NER methods ineffective
for the judicial documents.
Although existing NER methods achieve great success in many domains, most of
them normally ignore the nested nature of named entities (Wang et al. 2022). There
are some researches on the nested NER task by applying deep learning models in
resent years, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), attention mechanism, and
neural layered model. Ju et al. (2018) proposed a novel neural model to identify
nested entities by dynamically stacking flat NER layers. Each flat NER layer is based
on a bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) layer to capture sequential
context representation and a Conditional Random Field (CRF) decoder. Fei et al.
(2020) introduced a dispatched attention neural model with multi-task learning for

Wang et al. (2020) presented Pyramid, a novel layered model for nested NER. In the

There are probably only few methods are effective in the Chinese judicial NER

streams (Li et al. 2020): 1) Rule-based approaches, which do not need annotated data
as they rely on hand-crafted rules; 2) Statistic-based methods, including conditional

Rule-based and statistic-based methods are very corpus-dependent and the experts

and incomplete dictionaries, high precision and low recall are often observed from
these methods, and they cannot be transferred to other domains (Li et al. 2020). In
recent years, with the development of deep learning, deep-learning NER models have
received more attention and achieved better results. However, deep-learning methods
predict each character with a given feature independently without considering the
previously predicted labels, which may lead to invalid predicted label sequences and
thus reduce the accuracy of entity recognition results (Tang et al. 2020). These NER
methods mentioned above are rarely applied to the judicial field.

Based on these problems, this paper proposes a new NER model Adversarial

--- CONCLUSION ---
In this paper, we propose AT-Flat, an adversarial training model integrates task-shared
word boundary information from CWS to improve Chinese judgment documents
NER performance, and combines character and word information to get more legal
entities information. Extensive experiments based on two different Chinese judgment
document datasets and three public Chinese NER datasets show that AT-Flat achieves
the best performance compared to the state-of-the-art models. For future work, We
will adjust the model to expand its application fields.
Although AT-Flat achieves strong performance, it still has some limitations. One
potential bottleneck is its limited capacity to model long-range dependencies, which
may affect its effectiveness when processing lengthy legal documents. Additionally,

--- INTRODUCTION ---
In recent years, pretrained language models (PLMs) have achieved
remarkable progress in open-domain tasks for high-resource languages.
However, owing to the severe scarcity of both general-domain and
domain-speciﬁc corpora, low-resource languages still exhibit limited capability in domain-speciﬁc text mining, such as in the medical and legal
ﬁelds. Cross-lingual semantic alignment models facilitate the sharing
and utilization of domain data across multiple languages, providing an
eﬀective and low-cost approach to improve the performance of deep
learning models for low-resource languages in speciﬁc domains. Therefore, exploring cross-lingual semantic alignment models tailored for
low-resource languages has become increasingly important in domainspeciﬁc scenarios.
Current mainstream approaches to cross-lingual semantic alignment
can be broadly categorized into three types: cross-lingual ﬁne-tuning,

cross-lingual mapping, and cross-lingual knowledge distillation. These
methods enhance the semantic alignment capability of multilingual
models at the sentence level, word level, and intermodel level.
Cross-lingual ﬁne-tuning methods aim to align sentence-level semantics by optimizing the [CLS] representation in multilingual PLMs.
To further improve sentence alignment, researchers have introduced
auxiliary token-level alignment tasks during training (Hu et al., 2020;
Li et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023), and some studies leverage word
alignment supervision to increase sentence-level performance (Artetxe
et al., 2017b; Chousa et al., 2020; Lample et al., 2017; Lian et al., 2020;
Miao et al., 2024; Schwenk et al., 2019). Recently, Zhu et al. (2024)
argued that hard token-to-token alignment may disrupt sentence-level
structure and thus proposed the use of alignment matrices to preserve
contextual dependencies, coupled with a self-distillation mechanism to
improve model robustness. To further improve multilingual embedding
alignment for low-resource languages, Ai and Huang (2024) proposed

along with a systematic search for the optimal vocabulary size. Li and

Cross-lingual mapping methods focus on word-level semantic
alignment by learning a mapping matrix between word embeddings to
project diﬀerent languages into a shared representation space. This strategy is particularly well suited for low-resource languages (Artetxe et al.,
2018; Mikolov et al., 2013; Ruder et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2019). The
approach was ﬁrst introduced by Mikolov et al. (2013), and subsequent
studies have incorporated adversarial training mechanisms to iteratively
optimize the mapping matrix between a generator and a discriminator via parallel or contrastive corpora (Bai et al., 2019; Conneau et al.,
2017; Liang et al., 2023; Tao et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2017). Although
such methods support weakly supervised or even unsupervised learning, the adversarial mechanism is often sensitive to initialization and
prone to instability during training, especially with respect to agglutinative languages with complex morphological structures. Recently, Hu
and Xu (2024) proposed aligning multiple subspaces to alleviate structural inconsistency in the embedding space, exhibited in improvements
in medium-resource languages. This method struggles to address semantic misalignment caused by syntactic and morphological variations.
Cross-lingual knowledge distillation methods transfer semantic
knowledge from high-resource languages to low-resource models via a
teacher-student architecture. Typically, a high-resource PLM serves as
the teacher to guide the student model in learning task-speciﬁc semantics and achieving cross-lingual representation alignment (Ding et al.,
2022; Reimers & Gurevych, 2020; Wang et al., 2023). Unlike the previous two methods, knowledge distillation requires no large-scale labeled
data in the target language and oﬀers high transfer eﬃciency. It also enables the direct use of high-resource language representations in vertical domains to improve task performance for low-resource languages. In
addition, the distillation mechanism naturally supports model compression, making it more suitable for deployment. Despite its theoretical advantages, the practical eﬀectiveness of cross-lingual knowledge distillation remains constrained by two major challenges. First, the distillation
process relies on high-quality domain-speciﬁc parallel corpora, which
are extremely scarce for low-resource languages in specialized ﬁelds
such as medicine and law. Second, the signiﬁcant structural diﬀerences
between the semantic spaces of the source and target languages often
lead to unstable knowledge transfer. To alleviate the problem of semantic space heterogeneity, prior studies have explored techniques such as
prototype modeling, similarity-based constraints, and contrastive learning (Ge et al., 2023; Park et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2023). For example,
the IMASCL (Park et al., 2024) method addresses semantic space divergence by employing a soft contrastive learning strategy. Speciﬁcally,
this method distills the similarity structure derived from a monolingual
teacher model to guide the student model in aligning semantic representations across multiple languages. These methods perform well when applied to languages with similar syntactic and part-of-speech structures.
Their alignment eﬀectiveness remains limited when dealing with agglutinative languages that exhibit rich morphological variations and complex structures.
These challenges are particularly pronounced in China’s multi-ethnic
linguistic context. With the deepening of cultural integration, traditional
Chinese medicine has evolved into a comprehensive system that includes Tibetan, Miao, Mongolian, and other ethnic medical traditions.
Similarly, domains such as law and education also widely adopt minority languages. However, many of these languages—such as Mongolian,
Tibetan, Kazakh, and Uyghur—suﬀer from severe data scarcity, strong
agglutinative morphology, and ﬂexible word order, which further complicate semantic modeling and cross-lingual knowledge transfer.

In summary, current cross-lingual semantic alignment for domainspeciﬁc tasks in low-resource agglutinative languages faces three core
challenges: 1) Insuﬃcient data coverage: The insuﬃcient corpus coverage of low-resource languages (e.g., Mongolian) in multilingual pretraining, coupled with the scarcity of large-scale domain-parallel data,
signiﬁcantly hinders cross-lingual semantic alignment and domain
knowledge transfer. 2) Semantic space heterogeneity: Signiﬁcant differences in syntactic structures and lexical morphology between source
and target languages lead to inconsistencies in semantic representation
spaces, increasing the diﬃculty of alignment. 3) Strong agglutinative
morphology: Agglutinative languages such as Mongolian exhibit complex word formation and rich inﬂection, which further exacerbates data
sparsity and the out-of-vocabulary (OOV) problem.
Therefore, the objective of this study is to develop a cross-lingual
semantic alignment model tailored for low-resource languages that
can eﬀectively address three key issues-domain-speciﬁc data scarcity,
semantic space heterogeneity, and data sparsity caused by complex
morphology-while ensuring model robustness.
To this end, we proposes CLWKD, a cross-lingual mapping and
knowledge distillation framework for multilevel semantic alignment in
low-resource scenarios. The overall model architecture of CLWKD is illustrated in Fig. 1. Compared with existing approaches, CLWKD not only
introduces a multi-granularity collaborative strategy for semantic alignment, but also, for the ﬁrst time, combines a general-domain mapping
matrix with domain-speciﬁc knowledge distillation to address modeling
challenges in extremely low-resource language settings. The key components are as follows:
First, to alleviate the problem of domain-speciﬁc data scarcity, we
introduce a domain-speciﬁc pretrained model from a high-resource language as the teacher network. Using a general-domain mapping matrix
and a small amount of domain parallel data as a bridge, we eﬀectively
transfer and align domain knowledge to a multilingual PLM. Unlike

Works in NER are commonly based on newspaper text data or social media messages. Legal texts (e.g., opinions, rulings, and

capture fine-grained semantic information underlying legal texts.
As an illustration, in an STF ruling, a Justice’s name tagged as a ‘person’ in the text refers to one of possibly four different roles:
(i) the Justice who wrote the current opinion, (ii) the Justice who wrote a past decision (precedent) cited in the current the ruling,
(iii) the author of an academic work cited by the opinion, and (iv) a Justice who is part of the collegiate body deciding the case

influential Justices, we should look at each of those meanings separately in order to attribute the proper weights.
In this paper, we present a case study on the fine-grained annotation task of court rulings by law students, in which two levels of
nested legal entities were annotated. Nested entities mapped in a preliminary study composed of four coarser legal named entities
and twenty-four nested ones (fine-grained). In that study, we took into account findings in previous legal research produced with
our research team’s support, such as Falcão et al. (2019), Pereira et al. (2020).
To ensure reliability and capture the nuances of legal reasoning, the annotation task had the collaboration of 95 law students from
the Escola de Direito do Rio de Janeiro da Fundação Getúlio Vargas (FGV-Direito Rio Law School). A collaboration that was possible
when aligning teaching and research: the task was performed as part of coursework of two disciplines focused on technology. The
annotation process was composed of three phases: two shorter training efforts and one longer final task. To measure the quality
of annotations and adjust the guidelines and instructions when needed, we used Cohen’s Kappa to measure the inter-annotator
agreement throughout the task.
Furthermore, for the annotation task, we assembled a set of 764 decisions published by the STF between 2009 and 2018.
The collection was carefully selected to ensure that it was somewhat representative of STF’s output, given the different origins

Despite these advancements, several concerns remain. Firstly, generative LLMs such as OpenAI’s GPT-4, primarily built on
Transformer decoders and trained autoregressively, are inherently aligned with text generation patterns (Achiam et al., 2023).
However, NER necessitates context-sensitive sequence understanding, which may not be optimally addressed by directly employing
these decoder-based LLMs (DLLMs). While fine-tuning DLLMs can align them more closely with NER, such adjustment often comes at
the cost of their generality (Qi et al., 2023).
Secondly, ICL, which includes prompt engineering techniques such as Chain-of-Thought (Kocoń et al., 2023; Triguero et al., 2024),
can substantially harness the powerful reasoning capabilities of DLLMs. However, its effectiveness heavily depends on the quality and
variety of the demonstrations provided (Wang et al., 2023b). Current methods still exhibit limitations; for example, GPT-NER selects
demonstrations from the training set that are semantically closest to the test set, but overlooks the issue of meaning redundancy among
demonstrations. This redundancy restricts performance improvements, as, ideally, demonstrations should expose DLLMs to diverse
contexts. When multiple demonstrations convey essentially the same information, they fail to provide DLLMs with new insights,
instead offering redundant knowledge that could have been communicated in a single demonstration. This redundancy reduces the
overall amount of useful information DLLMs can learn, thereby diminishing its effectiveness.
Thirdly, existing research has not yet fully explored how ICL prompts can more effectively guide DLLMs to comprehend given texts.
A deeper understanding of the text by DLLMs could lead to more effective sequence-related processing, which remains an area ripe for
exploration.
To address the first challenge, we propose to harness the strengths of both encoder-based LLMs (ELLMs) and DLLMs for NER.
ELLMs, such as Microsoft’s DeBERTa-v2, may have fewer parameters relative to DLLMs, but their architecture - centered around
Transformer encoders trained with masked language modeling, makes them inherently more suitable for NER. Specifically, we propose
two complementary strategies. (a) We use DLLMs to extend the training dataset for ELLMs. Based on the original input, DLLMs can
generate additional contextually-rich text, and potentially introduce new knowledge and insights that are inherent to DLLMs, which is
beneficial for the training of ELLMs (Kocoń et al., 2023). (b) We combine the predictions from DLLMs and ELLMs to capitalize their
respective strength. By doing so, we can leverage the context sensitivity of ELLMs and the inferential prowess of DLLMs, allowing them
to contribute distinctively to the NER process.
For the second challenge, our focus is to ensure that demonstrations are not only semantically relevant but also free from
redundancy in meaning. To ascertain semantic relevance, we employ the commonly-used and effective method of embedding simi­
larity, with the embeddings being generated by ELLMs. To address redundancy, a straightforward approach is to evaluate potential
information overlap from the perspective of textual entailment. Inspired by the DLLMs’ adeptness at managing meaning relationships
(Farquhar et al., 2024), we leverage DLLMs to ascertain whether there is an entailment - meaning consistency - between demon­
strations. Building on this, we introduce a new demonstration retriever, SemnRank, which integrates ELLM-based semantic compu­
tations with DLLM-based bidirectional meaning entailment detection.
To tackle the third challenge, we draw on insights from information laws, which delineate deterministic patterns between the
macro-structure of text (the text itself) and its micro-components, such as words and characters. These laws provide a more funda­
mental understanding of the textual system (Stanisz et al., 2024). This framework is particularly enlightening across both natural and
social sciences, such as Zipf’s law. This law encapsulates the principle of least effort in information transmission, manifesting as a
quantifiable phenomenon where word frequency is inversely proportional to its ranking (DeMarzo et al., 2021). Therefore, we propose
a new prompting method, InforLaw-thought, which leverages information laws to guide DLLMs in processing a given text. By modeling
the text through the lens of information laws, DLLMs can gain a better understanding of the content.

--- ABSTRACT ---
Named Entity Recognition (NER) is a natural language processing task that traditionally relies on supervised learning and annotated data. Acquiring such data is often a challenge, particularly in specialized fields
like medical, legal, and financial sectors. Those are commonly referred to as low-resource domains, which
comprise long-tail entities, due to the scarcity of available data. To address this, data augmentation techniques are increasingly being employed to generate additional training instances from the original dataset.
In this study, we evaluate the effectiveness of two prominent text augmentation techniques, Mention
Replacement and Contextual Word Replacement, on two widely used NER models, Bi-LSTM + CRF and

with different quantities in order to fine-tune their projects.

Named Entity Recognition (NER) is a specialized subtask of Information Extraction that focuses
on identifying and categorizing mentions of real-world entities in text (Jurafsky and Martin 2000;
Li, Shang, and Chen 2021; Li et al. 2022a). Common entity types such as Person, Location, and
Organization are frequently encountered in abundant online sources such as news, websites, and
social media, and thus form what are called generic domains. These domains have been extensively
studied, and numerous datasets are readily available for research (Li et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2021a).
In contrast, entities in specialized application domains, also called low-resource domains (Dai
and Adel 2020; Liu, Chen, and Xu 2022), like medical, legal, and financial sectors, are termed as
long-tail entities (Cao et al. 2020; Liu et al. 2020). These domains are less commonly found in typical online text corpora. Additionally, the annotation of these specialized entities often demands
technical expertise, making the generation of annotated datasets a challenging task (Hou et al.
2020; Friedrich et al. 2020). Given the critical role of annotated data in the development, training,
and testing of NER models (Li 2022; Au, Lampos, and Cox 2022), there is a growing interest in

methodologies for building NER models in scenarios where annotated data are scarce or difficult

One such methodology is the application of data augmentation, which is a technique that

training data is important in the practical field of NLP. In this study, we examine the effectiveness
of two specific data augmentation techniques, Mention Replacement (MR) and Contextual Word

In this paper, we investigate the effectiveness of two representative data augmentation techniques, MR (Dai and Adel 2020) and CWR (Jiao et al. 2020), when used for NER tasks on
low-resource domain datasets, which contain long-tail entities. In our study, we use two popular
neural architectures for NER, Bi-LSTM + CRF and BERT, to generate several different NER models with distinct configurations. For training, these configurations used subsets of varying sizes of
the original datasets.
Specifically, we generated 532 groups of 10 models, from which we measure the average F1score and pick the best one for each subset. This yields 76 averages, 10 of which are considered
statistically significant improvements after using text augmentation. As these improved models
were those trained using smaller subsets, our study further supports the notion that data augmentation for NER models is more effective for smaller training sets than for larger ones. We also

verify that there is no predetermined optimal number of augmented instances that will certainly
improve the model. Not only that, but also augmentation also reduces model quality in several
other cases. Thus, NER practitioners should test different amounts, especially if the augmentation technique is prone to introducing spurious instances in the training data. We also show that,
for the datasets studied in this paper, CWR yields models that are better than MR, and that data
augmentation suits BERT NER models better than Bi-LSTM + CRF.
Our work on augmentation for NER in low-resource domains indicates that this landscape can
still be improved in both the case of top-notch but high-demanding architectures, such as BERT, or
in the case of competitive but less-demanding architectures, such as Bi-LSTM + CRF. While our
study provides valuable insights into the effectiveness of data augmentation techniques for NER
on low-resource domains, it also highlights several limitations that warrant further investigation.
Firstly, our findings show that data augmentation can introduce noise into the training data,
which can potentially degrade the quality of the NER models. This is particularly evident in our
experiments with larger subsets of the datasets, where data augmentation often resulted in lower
average F1-scores compared to models trained without augmented data. This suggests that while
data augmentation can help to increase the quantity of training data, it may also compromise its
quality, leading to overfitting or poor generalization to unseen data.
To mitigate this issue, future research could explore more sophisticated or controlled augmentation techniques that minimize the introduction of noise. For example, one could consider
techniques that take into account the semantic consistency between the original and augmented
sentences, or that adjust the degree of augmentation based on the complexity or diversity of the
dataset.
Secondly, our study assumes that all entities are equally important and should be augmented
at the same rate. However, in many real-world applications, some entities may be more critical
or rare than others, and therefore might benefit more from augmentation. Future work could
investigate differential augmentation strategies that prioritize certain entity types based on their
importance or rarity.
Lastly, our study focuses on two specific NER architectures (Bi-LSTM + CRF and BERT) and
two text augmentation techniques (MR and CWR). While these methods are widely used and have
shown promising results in many NLP tasks, they may not be optimal or applicable for all types
of NER problems or domains. Future research should consider exploring other architectures and
techniques (e.g., knowledge graphs to suggest new entities), as well as hybrid or ensemble methods that combine the strengths of multiple approaches. Also, as future work, we intend to explore
the use of generative models, particularly large language models (LLMs), due to recent experiments adopting them in NER. We aim to implement LLMs not only as advanced NER models
but also as innovative data augmentation techniques. By leveraging the generative capabilities of
these models, we anticipate enhancing the robustness and accuracy of our NER systems while
simultaneously enriching our datasets. This dual approach could lead to improved performance
in diverse NER tasks and better adaptability to various domains.
