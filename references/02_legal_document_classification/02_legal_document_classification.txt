--- CONCLUSION ---
In this paper, we presented LegalVis, a web-based visual
analytic system designed to assist lawyers and other judicial
experts in analyzing legal documents that cite or could
potentially cite binding precedents. LegalVis first identifies
potential citations by implementing a simple yet powerful
machine learning model based on classification. Then, an
interpretability mechanism also incorporated into the system
provides reliable explanations for the model’s decisions.

Finally, all this information becomes accessible through the
three interactive and linked views that compose the system.
Qualitative and quantitative analyses validated the performance of the proposed model and two usage scenarios demonstrated the usefulness and effectiveness of the LegalVis
system. Both scenarios were validated by six domain experts
not involved in the system’s development, who also reported
positive feedback.

--- TITLE ---
Article Legal Judgment Prediction in the Saudi Arabian Commercial Court Ashwaq Almalki, Safa Alsafari *

--- ABSTRACT ---
Legal judgment prediction is an emerging application of artificial intelligence in the legal
domain, offering significant potential to enhance legal decision support systems. Such
systems can improve judicial efficiency, reduce burdens on legal professionals, and assist in
early-stage case assessment. This study focused on predicting whether a legal case would
be Accepted or Rejected using only the Fact section of court rulings. A key challenge lay
in processing long legal documents, which often exceeded the input length limitations of
transformer-based models. To address this, we proposed a two-step methodology: first,
each document was segmented into sentence-level inputs compatible with AraBERT—a
pretrained Arabic transformer model—to generate sentence-level predictions; second, these
predictions were aggregated to produce a document-level decision using several methods,
including Mean, Max, Confidence-Weighted, and Positional aggregation. We evaluated
the approach on a dataset of 19,822 real-world cases collected from the Saudi Arabian
Commercial Court. Among all aggregation methods, the Confidence-Weighted method
applied to the AraBERT-based classifier achieved the highest performance, with an overall
accuracy of 85.62%. The results demonstrated that combining sentence-level modeling with
effective aggregation methods provides a scalable and accurate solution for Arabic legal
judgment prediction, enabling full-length document processing without truncation.

--- CONCLUSION ---
This study proposed a two-step framework for LJP in Arabic commercial court cases.
The Fact section of each legal document was first segmented into sentence-level inputs and
classified using AraBERT v2. Then, sentence-level predictions were aggregated to generate
a final document-level decision.
Experimental results on the test set demonstrated that all aggregation methods performed competitively, with Confidence-Weighted aggregation achieving the highest accuracy (85.62%) and F1-score (85.61%). Error analysis revealed higher precision for Rejected
cases, increased error rates in longer documents, and performance variations across case
types. These findings highlight the potential of aggregation-based methods for modeling
long Arabic legal documents, while also revealing persistent challenges in classifying
Accepted outcomes.
The proposed framework could serve as a reliable LJP assistant, enhancing judicial
efficiency by reducing case screening time and providing early insights into likely verdicts.
In practice, such a system may assist judges and lawyers by estimating potential outcomes
based on historical patterns. However, it should be viewed strictly as a decision-support
tool rather than a replacement for human judgment, as final legal decisions must remain
the responsibility of judges.
This work also addresses a critical research gap by enabling large-scale modeling of
long Arabic legal documents and providing interpretable, outcome-focused predictions.
Unlike prior approaches that focused on narrower tasks, smaller datasets, or different legal
domains, our method targets binary judgment prediction for commercial court cases using
a large dataset of 19,822 documents. These contributions advance Arabic LJP by providing
a scalable, accurate, and practical framework tailored to the Saudi judicial context.
Although the results provide an advancement over existing studies, they underscore
the need for further refinement and expansion. Future research should focus on developing models capable of capturing long-range context in Arabic legal texts and extending
evaluation to diverse legal domains, thereby enhancing generalizability and applicability
in real-world judicial settings. In addition, future work could address more complex prediction tasks, such as handling cases labeled as “Mixed” where multiple outcomes occur
within the same judgment, or extracting the specific legal articles referenced in the court’s
decision. Finally, incorporating domain-adaptive pretraining using large-scale Arabic
legal corpora could further improve the model’s ability to understand legal terminology
and reasoning.

--- ABSTRACT ---
Official gazettes are documents published by governments to publicize their actions, spanning long periods of
time and making an important transparency mechanism. These documents have information on laws, contracts,
and bidding processes, as well as on civil servants and their careers in public service. Automatic information
extraction of these documents may contribute to public transparency, with two tasks being especially useful: the
classification of the different segments of these documents, the so called acts; and the Named Entity Recognition
(NER) within the acts. The variety of official gazettes and their patterns brings up the necessity of constructing
different tools for specific gazettes. In this paper, we propose DODFMiner, a command-line interface tool to
classify acts and extract named entities from the Official Gazette of the Federal District. The tool follows a
3-step approach: the pre-processing of the input data; text classification using rule-based systems with regular
expressions; and NER with Machine Learning algorithms. It allows users to input JSON files and receive CSV
as output, providing information that allows users to track government procurements through years, contracts
duration and total amount, among others. We also propose a set of experiments to support the choice of
models included in the tool, covering the classification and NER steps. Text classification achieved a mean
F1-score of 0.778, while to the NER, we compared 3 different architectures, CRF with a mean F1-score of
0.851, CNN-biLSTM-CRF with 0.787 and CNN-CNN-LSTM with 0.841.

--- INTRODUCTION ---
Official gazettes are periodical publications that aim to publicize
governments legal actions, having information on contracts, civil servants, procurements, published laws, among others. They are widely
used worldwide as recorded by the Foreign Official Gazette Database,1
counting up to 663 active publications in 2007. These are periodical
publications that frequently cover large periods of time, with notable
examples being The London Gazette, published in the United Kingdom
since 1665, and the Buletín Oficial Del Estado, since 1661 in Spain. In
Brazil, the Official Diary of the Union (ODU) has been published since
1862 and all the federative units have their respective official gazettes,
making a total of 27 publications. Municipalities can also have their

own gazettes, such as is the case of the cities São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro,
Feira de Santana, among others.
They constitute an important source of information to the society,
and have also historical importance due to the periodicity of the
publication. The right of public access to legal information makes
governments responsible for publishing legal information online in an
accessible way [1]. Providing data in an accessible way empowers
citizens to build their own tools to track government’s actions, being
able, for example, to track governments and politicians’ spending to
identify irregularities [2].
In Brazil, the Federal District has its own official gazette, the Official
Gazette of the Federal District (OGFD).2 It is composed of a series of
reports of government actions and is published on a nearly daily basis.

--- INTRODUCTION ---
In this digital age, the rapid growth of digital and online publications
has led to an overwhelming number of documents being published daily.
Therefore, it is increasingly important to organize document data
effectively. In this context, document classification has emerged as a
significant concept and has attracted widespread attention in various
fields, such as spam detection (Fahfouh, Riffi, Mahraz, Yahyaouy, &
Tairi, 2022), news recommendation (Liao et al., 2022), and legal
document classification (Guo, Zhang, & Tian, 2023).
Document classification is heavily dependent on extracting features
that can effectively represent the documents. Traditional methods, such
as the bag-of-words or term frequency-inverse document frequency (TFIDF), have been widely used. However, these methods rely on hand­
crafted features, which are labor-intensive and struggle to fully capture

the semantics of a document. The advancements in deep learning
methods have led to the development of more sophisticated approaches
which can utilize contextual information, such as Word2Vec (Mikolov,
Chen, Corrado, & Dean, 2013), RNNs (Mikolov, Karafí at, Burget, Cer­
nock‘y, and Khudanpur, 2010), and CNNs (Kim, 2014; Zhang, Zhao, &
LeCun, 2015). The effectiveness of these methods largely depends on
their ability to extract context from word sequences. More recently,
graph-based methods have gained considerable attention for their
impressive performance in document classification tasks (Yao, Mao, &
Luo, 2019; Liu et al., 2022). These approaches transform documents into
graphs to capture topological structure, in which each word serves as a
node, and the relation between words forms the edges. Then, graph
neural networks (Kipf & Welling, 2017) are used to extract long-distance
interactions between words.

2.2. Graph-based document classification
Graph-based models can capture the topological structure of docu­
ments. Recently, numerous graph-based approaches have been inte­
grated into NLP tasks, providing innovative solutions for various tasks,
particularly document classification. Graph methods can be divided into
two types, transductive and inductive methods.
Transductive graph-based classification methods utilize both labeled
and unlabeled data in the learning phase. TextGCN (Yao et al., 2019)
build a single document graph for corpus based on word co-occurrence
and document word relations, then learn the document graph by a graph
convolutional network. TensorGCN (Liu, You, Zhang, Wu, & Lv, 2020)
captured semantic, syntactic, and word-cooccurrence features of the
document to model its content. Bijari, Zare, Kebriaei, and Veisi (2020)
proposed to utilize a sentence-level graph-based text representation
which includes stop words to consider semantic and term relations for
classification. ME-GCN (Wang, Han, Long, & Poon, 2022) considered the
rich edge information of the document graph. HGCN (Liu et al., 2022)
utilized information from two elements, including section and word
information, with a decoupled graph convolution block to conduct
document classification. Wu (2023) proposed utilizing text-related
commonsense knowledge as a priori knowledge in semi-supervised
short text classification. These methods can leverage global informa­
tion from documents but require retraining on the entire graph when
new data is added. Thus, they are limited in practical application.
Inductive graph-based classification methods utilize labeled data in
the learning phase and can be easily generalized to new data without
additional training. These methods typically require a graph readout
module to extract document representation for final classification. For
instance, TextING (Zhang, Yu, Cui, Wu, Wen, & Wang, 2020) build in­
dividual graphs for each document based on word co-occurrence and
used gated graph neural networks to learn node representations. This
method applied an attention readout module on the node to derive
document representations. HyperGAT (Ding, Wang, Li, Li, & Liu, 2020)
constructed a document-level hypergraph to capture word cooccurrence and used hypergraph learning to extract high-order infor­
mation in documents. It applied mean pooling on node representations
to obtain document representation. GFN (Dai et al., 2022) constructed
multiple homogeneous document graphs and merges different text
graph perspectives for enhanced structural insight into the model.

graph structure. It used sum pooling on node representations to obtain
document representation. Nguyen et al. (2022) proposed incorporating
a transformer module into the graph model to capture word position

6. Limitation analysis and future direction
Despite the superior performance of MHGAT demonstrated in the
experimental results, we have also observed some limitations. In this
section, we elaborate on these limitations and suggest future directions,
as shown below:
1) Through the analysis of the impact of pre-trained word embed­
dings, it has been observed that commonly used embeddings such as
GloVe and Word2Vec cannot enhance the performance of MHGAT. This
limitation may arise because these embeddings are optimized for
sequential models and may not align well with the information propa­
gation mechanisms inherent to graph models. Therefore, exploring pretrained word embeddings that are specifically designed for graph-based
document classification models is a valuable direction for future
research.
2) The analysis revealed that MHGAT is sensitive to hyperparameters, suggesting that additional effort may be required to deter­
mine optimal settings for real-world applications. Exploring methods for
adaptively optimizing model hyper-parameters could enhance the
automation of the document classification system, making it a valuable
direction for future research.
3) The analysis of layer numbers revealed that MHGAT struggles
with the over-smoothing problem, which could potentially limit its
expressive power. Therefore, exploring methods to handle this issue is
an interesting direction for future research.
4) The analysis of training data ratios demonstrated that MHGAT
requires more labeled training data than the transductive comparison
model, which may increase the cost of data collection in practical ap­
plications. Therefore, exploring how to utilize training data more
effectively is another valuable research direction.
