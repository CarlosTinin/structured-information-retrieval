===== PAPER: 02_01_LegalVis_Exploring_and_Inferring_Precedent_Citations_in_Legal_Documents =====
This paper presents LegalVis, a web-based visual analytics system designed to help legal experts explore and analyze citations to binding precedents in Brazilian Supreme Court (STF) decisions. The system combines a machine learning classification model for identifying potential (implicit) citations with an interpretability mechanism and multiple linked interactive views for document exploration. Validated through two usage scenarios and feedback from six domain experts, LegalVis demonstrates how ML-based document classification can be integrated with visual analytics to address the challenge of navigating over one million STF decisions. The work is relevant to legal document classification as it applies supervised learning to infer implicit legal references within lengthy judicial documents.

===== PAPER: 02_03_Legal Judgment Prediction in the Saudi Arabian Commercial Court =====
This study proposes a two-step framework for legal judgment prediction in Arabic commercial court cases, addressing the challenge of processing long legal documents that exceed transformer input limits. The methodology segments documents into sentence-level inputs classified by AraBERT, then aggregates predictions to produce document-level decisions using methods including Mean, Max, Confidence-Weighted, and Positional aggregation. Evaluated on 19,822 real-world cases from the Saudi Arabian Commercial Court, the Confidence-Weighted method achieved the highest accuracy of 85.62%. The work contributes to Arabic legal NLP by enabling full-length document processing without truncation and demonstrating the viability of aggregation-based approaches for long document classification.

===== PAPER: 02_04_A_Multi-Modal_Approach_to_Digital_Document_Stream_Segmentation_for_Title_Insurance_Domain =====
This paper proposes a multi-modal deep learning approach for Document Stream Segmentation (DSS) in the Title Insurance domain, combining image features from VGG16 with text features from Legal-BERT for page-level classification of first pages versus other pages. The authors address limitations of prior DSS architectures that relied on simpler text feature extractors, demonstrating that incorporating BERT-based transfer learning for text representation significantly improves segmentation accuracy on proprietary legal and property documents. The proposed model was deployed in a production Intelligent Document Processing System, achieving superior performance over baseline architectures. This work is relevant to legal document processing pipelines as it addresses the fundamental task of automatically segmenting multi-page document streams prior to classification and extraction.

===== PAPER: 02_05_DODFMiner- An automated tool for Named Entity Recognitio =====
This paper presents DODFMiner, a command-line tool for automatically classifying acts and extracting named entities from the Official Gazette of the Federal District of Brazil (OGFD). The tool follows a three-step pipeline: pre-processing, rule-based text classification (achieving F1-score of 0.778), and NER using machine learning algorithms, where CRF achieved the best F1-score of 0.851 compared to CNN-biLSTM-CRF (0.787) and CNN-CNN-LSTM (0.841). The system enables tracking of government procurements, contracts, and civil servant careers, contributing to public transparency. This work demonstrates how combining rule-based classification with ML-based NER can effectively process structured legal documents in Portuguese.

===== PAPER: 02_06_Multi-label legal document classification- A deep learning-based =====
This paper addresses multi-label classification of legal documents by introducing the POSTURE50K dataset (50K annotated legal cases with procedural postures) and proposing LAMT_MLC, a deep learning system built on RoBERTa with continued pre-training, label-attention mechanisms, and multi-task learning to handle low-frequency classes. The dataset follows a Zipfian distribution making it particularly challenging, with many labels having very few samples. The proposed system substantially outperforms baseline and state-of-the-art methods on both POSTURE50K and EUROLEX57 datasets, particularly in Macro-F1 coverage of rare classes. This work is highly relevant to legal NLP as it addresses the real-world problem of classifying legal documents into fine-grained categories with highly imbalanced label distributions.

===== PAPER: 02_07_A Meta Analysis of Attention Models on Legal Judgment Prediction System =====
This paper presents a meta-analysis of attention-based deep learning models, particularly Hierarchical Attention Networks (HAN) and Transformer architectures, applied to Legal Judgment Prediction (LJP). The authors review and classify recent LJP methods based on empirical approaches and conceptual frameworks for text classification, discussing challenges of encoding lengthy legal documents and incorporating external legal information. The work focuses on showcasing how attention mechanisms can improve document representation for prediction tasks across different jurisdictions including Indian, UK, and Chinese courts. This review contributes to understanding the role of attention-based architectures in handling the unique challenges of legal text classification.

===== PAPER: 02_08_Classifying European Court of Human Right =====
This paper applies transformer-based models to classify legal cases from the European Court of Human Rights (ECHR) regarding human rights violations. The authors motivate the work by addressing the high cost of legal representation and the potential for automated case classification to improve efficiency and reduce costs in legal aid systems. Several transformer architectures are evaluated for classifying lengthy ECHR documents, building on prior work in legal text classification. The study is relevant to legal NLP as it demonstrates how pre-trained language models can be adapted to handle the specific challenges of classifying complex human rights case documents.

===== PAPER: 02_09_Capturing word positions does help- A multi-element hypergraph gated =====
This paper proposes MHGAT, a multi-element hypergraph gated attention network for document classification that addresses limitations of prior graph-based methods in capturing word position information and multi-element document structures. The model introduces a heterogeneous multi-element hypergraph (MHG) with sentence, content, and position hyperedges, combined with homogenization and gated attention modules for learning expressive document representations. Extensive experiments on five datasets demonstrate that MHGAT outperforms state-of-the-art methods including GPT-3.5 (achieving 97.65% vs 83.38% accuracy on the R8 dataset). While not specifically legal-focused, this work is relevant to legal document classification as it provides a novel inductive graph-based approach capable of handling the complex structural features characteristic of legal texts.
