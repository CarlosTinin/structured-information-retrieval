Over the last few years, graph-based methods have manifested a significant enhancement in document mining
applications such as spam detection, news recommendation, and legal document classification. However,
existing graph-based methods have a limited ability to utilize word position and multi-element information
within the documents, limiting their effectiveness in practical application. To mitigate this limitation, we propose
a novel multi-element hypergraph gated attention network that can capture word position and multi-element
information for accurate document classification. Specifically, a new multi-element hypergraph is first pro­
posed to describe the word position, sentence, and full content within the document. Then, a new multi-element
homogenization module is applied to mitigate heterogeneity of constructed hypergraph. Meantime, a new
hypergraph gated attention module is proposed to filter noise in the constructed hypergraph and derive various
element representations that incorporate word position information. Finally, a new block-wise read-out module
is designed to fuse learned element representations into comprehensive document representations for classifi­
cation. Extensive experiments conducted on several real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed method
not only outperforms related state-of-the-art methods but is also faster, making it suitable for a wide range of
practical applications. For instance, our method achieved an accuracy improvement of 1.1 % over the best
comparative method on some datasets while also operating at a faster speed. Additionally, it demonstrated an
impressive 14 % improvement in accuracy over the well-known Generative Pre-trained Transformer 3.5 (GPT3.5) on one dataset.

1. Introduction
In this digital age, the rapid growth of digital and online publications
has led to an overwhelming number of documents being published daily.
Therefore, it is increasingly important to organize document data
effectively. In this context, document classification has emerged as a
significant concept and has attracted widespread attention in various
fields, such as spam detection (Fahfouh, Riffi, Mahraz, Yahyaouy, &
Tairi, 2022), news recommendation (Liao et al., 2022), and legal
document classification (Guo, Zhang, & Tian, 2023).
Document classification is heavily dependent on extracting features
that can effectively represent the documents. Traditional methods, such
as the bag-of-words or term frequency-inverse document frequency (TFIDF), have been widely used. However, these methods rely on hand­
crafted features, which are labor-intensive and struggle to fully capture

the semantics of a document. The advancements in deep learning
methods have led to the development of more sophisticated approaches
which can utilize contextual information, such as Word2Vec (Mikolov,
Chen, Corrado, & Dean, 2013), RNNs (Mikolov, Karafí at, Burget, Cer­
nock‘y, and Khudanpur, 2010), and CNNs (Kim, 2014; Zhang, Zhao, &
LeCun, 2015). The effectiveness of these methods largely depends on
their ability to extract context from word sequences. More recently,
graph-based methods have gained considerable attention for their
impressive performance in document classification tasks (Yao, Mao, &
Luo, 2019; Liu et al., 2022). These approaches transform documents into
graphs to capture topological structure, in which each word serves as a
node, and the relation between words forms the edges. Then, graph
neural networks (Kipf & Welling, 2017) are used to extract long-distance
interactions between words.

To tackle these challenges, we propose a novel multi-element
hypergraph gated attention network (MHGAT) that can learn compre­

hyperedge, and position hyperedge to capture local sentence informa­
tion, global content information, and specific word position informa­

applied to capture “deep” interactions of element features and mitigate
the element heterogeneity. Meanwhile, we propose a new hypergraph
gated attention module (HGA) to filter noise on constructed MHGs
through a gated mechanism and learn various element representations

2.2. Graph-based document classification
Graph-based models can capture the topological structure of docu­
ments. Recently, numerous graph-based approaches have been inte­
grated into NLP tasks, providing innovative solutions for various tasks,
particularly document classification. Graph methods can be divided into
two types, transductive and inductive methods.
Transductive graph-based classification methods utilize both labeled
and unlabeled data in the learning phase. TextGCN (Yao et al., 2019)
build a single document graph for corpus based on word co-occurrence
and document word relations, then learn the document graph by a graph
convolutional network. TensorGCN (Liu, You, Zhang, Wu, & Lv, 2020)
captured semantic, syntactic, and word-cooccurrence features of the
document to model its content. Bijari, Zare, Kebriaei, and Veisi (2020)
proposed to utilize a sentence-level graph-based text representation
which includes stop words to consider semantic and term relations for
classification. ME-GCN (Wang, Han, Long, & Poon, 2022) considered the
rich edge information of the document graph. HGCN (Liu et al., 2022)
utilized information from two elements, including section and word
information, with a decoupled graph convolution block to conduct
document classification. Wu (2023) proposed utilizing text-related
commonsense knowledge as a priori knowledge in semi-supervised
short text classification. These methods can leverage global informa­
tion from documents but require retraining on the entire graph when
new data is added. Thus, they are limited in practical application.
Inductive graph-based classification methods utilize labeled data in
the learning phase and can be easily generalized to new data without
additional training. These methods typically require a graph readout
module to extract document representation for final classification. For
instance, TextING (Zhang, Yu, Cui, Wu, Wen, & Wang, 2020) build in­
dividual graphs for each document based on word co-occurrence and
used gated graph neural networks to learn node representations. This
method applied an attention readout module on the node to derive
document representations. HyperGAT (Ding, Wang, Li, Li, & Liu, 2020)
constructed a document-level hypergraph to capture word cooccurrence and used hypergraph learning to extract high-order infor­
mation in documents. It applied mean pooling on node representations
to obtain document representation. GFN (Dai et al., 2022) constructed
multiple homogeneous document graphs and merges different text
graph perspectives for enhanced structural insight into the model.
TextSSL (Piao, Lee, Lee, & Kim, 2022) generated a document-level graph
to capture word co-occurrence and used structure learning to refine
graph structure. It used sum pooling on node representations to obtain
document representation. Nguyen et al. (2022) proposed incorporating
a transformer module into the graph model to capture word position
information. However, this approach still failed to capture the rich in­
formation inherent in elements beyond words and increased model
complexity. Tan and Wang (2022) proposed a directed graph with
labeled edges to model the word order based on word co-occurrence but
could only capture the order of every three words and could not utilize

2. Related work
2.1. Traditional document classification

cessing and has a wide range of applications. For the traditional docu­
ment classification methods, there are two typical research directions,
word-based methods and sequence-based methods.
Word-based methods focus on individual words or sentences as the
primary features for document classification. These methods often rely
on the frequency of terms in the documents and require specific feature
engineering to effectively represent the documents for classification
tasks. Wang, Sun, Zhang, and Li (2006) crafted an optimal SVM for
document categorization using weight definition and feature selection.
Farhoodi, Yari, and Sayah (2011) proposed a Persian document classi­
fication model based on word level n-grams.

3. Methodology
In this section, we first introduce the construction of the MHG.
Subsequently, we detail the components of MHGAT, which comprises
the MEH module, HGA module, and block-wise read-out module. Fig. 1
illustrates the framework and workflow of MHGAT.
3.1. Documents as multi-element hypergraph (MHG)
Previous graph-based document classification methods typically
employed a simple graph to describe the topological structure of docu­
ments. However, these approaches have two notable limitations. Firstly,
it fails to depict multiple elements within the document. Secondly, the
simple graph is difficult to capture the word position information within
the document. To overcome the aforementioned limitations, we propose
a novel heterogeneous hypergraph structure called MHG. The MHG
considers words as nodes and incorporates sentence hyperedges, content
hyperedges, and position hyperedges simultaneously. The design of the
MHG endows it with the ability to effectively capture different elements
and word position information within the document.

cannot utilize multiple element representations in multi-element
hypergraphs, such as sentence and content hyperedge representations.
Thus, we propose a block-wise readout that employs different readout
strategies for various element representations and fuses them into a
comprehensive document representation for accurate classification.

Our experiments employ four categories of baseline methods: wordbased methods, sequence-based methods, graph-based transductive
methods, and graph-based inductive methods.
In the category of word-based methods: we employ fasttext (Boja­
nowski, Grave, Joulin, & Mikolov, 2017) and WideMLP-2 (Galke &
Scherp, 2022). For sequence-based methods: we utilize TextCNN (Chen,
2015) and TextRNN (Liu, Qiu, & Huang, 2016). In the category of
graph-based transductive methods: we employ TextGCN (Yao et al.,
2019) and SGC (Wu, Souza, Zhang, Fifty, Yu, & Weinberger, 2019). For
graph-based inductive methods: we use TextING (Zhang et al., 2020),
HyperGAT (Ding et al., 2020), InductTGCN (Wang, Han, & Poon, 2022),
TextSSL (Piao et al., 2022), and UGformer (Nguyen, Nguyen, & Phung,
2022). We implement these methods using code from the official re­
positories provided by the authors. The text embeddings for TextCNN
and TextRNN were initialized using GloVe, as this approach demon­
strated better results. For the remaining methods, we adhered to the
prescribed official configurations.
To ensure a fair comparison, all methods in our experiments are
conducted ten times under identical conditions, while computing the
average accuracy and standard deviations. Each method is trained 10
epochs with an early stopping mechanism. Our experiments are con­
ducted on a Linux system equipped with a 4090 GPU and 32 GB of RAM,
using the PyTorch deep learning framework.
In our experiments, MHGAT employs a mini-batch size of 32 and is
trained using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001. To
reduce overfitting, we apply weight decay with a coefficient of 0.001
and enhance generalization by using a dropout rate of 0.3. In the MR
dataset, we initialize the word embedding with GloVe, while random
initialization is applied for the other datasets.

4.7. Computational efficiency analysis
We compare the computational complexity in terms of time of our
method with four inductive models on the five datasets using identical
experimental settings. Specifically, we set the mini-batch size to 32 and
the hidden dimension to 100. It can be seen from Table 5, the MHGAT
model has the fastest per-epoch training time in comparison with other
inductive methods on all datasets. This is attributed to the fact that MHG
does not require any padding operation, thereby improves the compu­
tational efficiency of downstream learning. Conversely, the HyperGAT
exhibits increased time consumption in the 20NG dataset, since this
method requires more calculation of padding character on long dataset.
This phenomenon also indicates that the time overhead of HyperGAT
can multiply with the length of the sentence or document. TextING and
UGformer are limited by memory usage and appear an out-of-memory
error, suggesting that they may not be suitable for handling larger
datasets. Overall, the results suggest that the MHGAT has the potential

In this article, we propose MHG, a new hypergraph structure for
document modeling. MHG captures various element information within
the document, including word position information. Then, a novel
MHGAT method is proposed to achieve the following: homogenize
heterogeneous hyperedges, filter out noise on the MHG, learn various
element representations that incorporate word position information and
multi-element information, and fuse learned representations for classi­
fication. Extensive experiments on five real-world document classifica­
tion datasets were performed to verify the superior performance of the
proposed metho. Through the comparative analysis of experimental
results, the following noteworthy conclusions are drawn:
1) The proposed MHGAT outperforms the state-of-the-art related
document classification methods, indicating that the proposed method
can effectively capture the sematic of document.
2) The proposed MEH module significantly enhances the perfor­
mance of MHGAT, contributing the most substantial improvement
across most datasets. This indicates that MEH can effectively mitigate
and capture “deep” interactions among heterogeneous hyperedge
information.
3) The various hyperedges in an MHG can complement one another.
Designing expressive hyperedges can further enhance the performance
of the MHGAT. Our future work will explore more diverse hyperedges.
In summary, the proposed document modeling method MHG and
classification method MHGAT show significant advantages, promising
substantial improvements for real-world document classification
applications.

6. Limitation analysis and future direction
Despite the superior performance of MHGAT demonstrated in the
experimental results, we have also observed some limitations. In this
section, we elaborate on these limitations and suggest future directions,
as shown below:
1) Through the analysis of the impact of pre-trained word embed­
dings, it has been observed that commonly used embeddings such as
GloVe and Word2Vec cannot enhance the performance of MHGAT. This
limitation may arise because these embeddings are optimized for
sequential models and may not align well with the information propa­
gation mechanisms inherent to graph models. Therefore, exploring pretrained word embeddings that are specifically designed for graph-based
document classification models is a valuable direction for future
research.
2) The analysis revealed that MHGAT is sensitive to hyperparameters, suggesting that additional effort may be required to deter­
mine optimal settings for real-world applications. Exploring methods for
adaptively optimizing model hyper-parameters could enhance the
automation of the document classification system, making it a valuable
direction for future research.
3) The analysis of layer numbers revealed that MHGAT struggles
with the over-smoothing problem, which could potentially limit its
expressive power. Therefore, exploring methods to handle this issue is
an interesting direction for future research.
4) The analysis of training data ratios demonstrated that MHGAT
requires more labeled training data than the transductive comparison
model, which may increase the cost of data collection in practical ap­
plications. Therefore, exploring how to utilize training data more
effectively is another valuable research direction.
