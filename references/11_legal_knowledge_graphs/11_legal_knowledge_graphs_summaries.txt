===== PAPER: 11_01_Design of Knowledge Graph Retrieval System for Legal andRegulatory Framework of Multilevel Latent Semantic Indexing =====
This paper designs a knowledge graph retrieval system for the Chinese legal domain by combining multilevel latent semantic indexing with graph database technology. The authors analyze the statistical properties of latent semantic spaces using Zipf's law and build a procuratorial affairs knowledge graph using Neo4j, implementing functions such as knowledge query, recommendation, and similar case retrieval. A bi-LSTM-CRF model is employed for named entity recognition in legal texts, using multi-channel inputs including word vectors and knowledge entity vectors to achieve significant improvement in crime prediction tasks. This work is relevant to legal NLP as it demonstrates an end-to-end approach combining NER with knowledge graphs for judicial case-handling assistance.

===== PAPER: 11_02_Steps Towards a Legal Ontological Turn Proposals for Law's Place beyond the Human =====
This paper proposes a "legal ontological turn" in environmental law, challenging the dominant "one-world world" paradigm that treats law as an independent set of norms regulating human use of the environment. Drawing on ecological jurisprudence, Indigenous legal thinking, and anthropological insight, the authors argue that current legal forms fail to prevent environmental harm because they specify allowable damage rather than adjudicating mutually enhancing relations. The paper seeks to ground future discussions toward building earth-sustaining environmental law for all beings. While not directly focused on NLP or information extraction, this work provides theoretical context for understanding diverse legal ontologies that inform knowledge representation in legal systems.

===== PAPER: 11_03_Populating legal ontologies using semantic role labeling =====
This paper addresses the "resource consumption bottleneck" of manually creating legal semantic technologies by developing a semantic role labeling-based information extraction system to extract definitions and norms from legislation. The system uses general-purpose SRL combined with dependency parse trees to identify norms, classify their types (obligations, powers, permissions), and map arguments to domain-specific slots in legal ontologies such as Eunomos and Legal-URN. The authors demonstrate extraction of various norm types including meta-norms indicating scope, hierarchy, and exceptions from EU legislation. This work is highly relevant to legal NLP as it presents a semi-automated pipeline for structuring legal text into machine-processable ontological representations, directly addressing challenges analogous to extracting structured information from judicial decisions.

===== PAPER: 11_04_Few-shot Named Entity Recognition- Definition, Taxonomy and Research Directions =====
This survey provides a comprehensive review of state-of-the-art algorithms for few-shot Named Entity Recognition, addressing the challenge of building NER systems when large labeled training corpora are unavailable. The authors propose a taxonomy dividing approaches into model-centric (meta-learning, transfer learning) and data-centric (active learning, distant supervision, self-learning) categories, analyzing advantages and limitations of each. A benchmark study of seven well-known few-shot learning methods for NER is included to establish baselines for future research. This work is relevant to legal NLP as it directly addresses the scenario of building NER systems for specialized legal domains where annotated data is scarce, which is a core challenge in extracting entities from judicial decisions.

===== PAPER: 11_05_Construction of Legal Knowledge Graph Based on Knowledge-Enhanced Large Language Models =====
This paper develops a knowledge-enhanced Large Language Model for constructing a Chinese Legal Knowledge Graph (CLKG), addressing the challenge of automatically extracting accurate knowledge from multiple heterogeneous legal sources. The authors implement a prefix fine-tuning strategy (JKEM) that embeds prior knowledge into the LLM while keeping main parameters frozen, achieving 90.92% accuracy under weakly supervised conditions. The resulting CLKG integrates 3,480 knowledge triples composed of 9 core entities and 2 key relationships, providing support for legal knowledge reasoning and decision-making. This work is relevant to legal NLP as it demonstrates how LLMs can be fine-tuned for domain-specific legal information extraction to populate structured knowledge representations.
